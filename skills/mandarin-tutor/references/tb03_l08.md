**陳敏萱：** 早，李文彥。欸？你怎麼看起來不太有精神？怎麼回事？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Zǎo, Lǐ Wényàn. Ēi? Nǐ zěnme kànqǐlái bú tài yǒu jīngshén? Zěnme huí shì?*

**李文彥：** 別說了。我那個室友，最近不是整夜開著燈打電玩，就是熬夜看球賽，激動的時候，還大喊大叫，害我睡不著覺。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Bié shuō le. Wǒ nàge shìyǒu, zuìjìn bú shì zhěngyè kāizhe dēng dǎ diànwán, jiù shì áoyè kàn qiúsài, jīdòng de shíhou, hái dàhǎn dàjiào, hài wǒ shuì bu zháo jiào.*

**李文彥：** 其實他對當公務員一點興趣都沒有，是他媽媽叫他去考的。他覺得很煩，所以最近常打電玩。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Qíshí tā duì dāng gōngwùyuán yìdiǎn xìngqù dōu méiyǒu, shì tā māma jiào tā qù kǎo de. Tā juéde hěn fán, suǒyǐ zuìjìn cháng dǎ diànwán.*

**陳敏萱：** 快要期中考了，我壓力好大啊！這學期我在大學選了好幾門課，還在語言中心上密集班，每天忙得連睡覺的時間都不夠。

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Kuàiyào qízhōng kǎo le, wǒ yālì hǎo dà a! Zhè xuéqī wǒ zài dàxué xuǎn le hǎo jǐ mén kè, hái zài yǔyán zhōngxīn shàng mìjí bān, měitiān máng de lián shuìjiào de shíjiān dōu bú gòu.*

**李文彥：** 這樣一來，妳不是就沒時間參加學校的社團活動了嗎？

*Lǐ Wényàn: Zhèyàng yì lái, nǐ bú shì jiù méi shíjiān cānjiā xuéxiào de shètuán huódòng le ma?*

**陳敏萱：** 那有什麼關係。我來台灣是來讀書的，不是來玩的。我希望早一點把中文系的學分修完，回國以後專心修輔系的課，這樣明年才能畢業。

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Nà yǒu shénme guānxi? Wǒ lái Táiwān shì lái dúshū de, bú shì lái wán de. Wǒ xīwàng zǎo yìdiǎn bǎ Zhōngwén Xì de xuéfēn xiū wán, huíguó yǐhòu zhuānxīn xiū fǔxì de kè, zhèyàng míngnián cái néng bìyè.*

## 閱讀 Reading

### 麵包冠軍吳寶春

2013年三月，世界麵包大賽冠軍吳寶春通過新加坡大學EMBA（高級管理人員工商管理碩士）研究所的面試，準備從七月起開始他的學生生活。吳寶春也申請過台灣的EMBA，卻因為只有國中畢業、學歷太低而被拒絕。「吳寶春要念研究所」在台灣引起了很多人的討論。有人認為學校太沒有彈性，時代已經改變了，制度也應該隨著改變；但是也有人認為他的基礎不夠，如果讓他念研究所，對認真念書的人不公平。

吳寶春出生在台灣南部鄉下，是家裡最小的孩子。他家很窮，父親很早就過世了。母親為了養八個孩子，每天辛苦地工作。吳寶春小時候不了解讀書的重要，不喜歡念書。按照他自己的說法，國中畢業的時候，他認識的字還不到五百個。

十七歲那一年，他離開家鄉，一個人到台北一家麵包店當學徒。工作的時候因為書念得不多，吃了很多苦。這時候他才明白讀書的重要。當兵的時候，他利用時間看電視認字。後來他因為想看懂日文烘焙書，去學日文。幾年以後，更因為工作的關係到日本學習，讓他大開眼界，了解到烘焙麵包不但是專業技術，也是一門藝術。

吳寶春經過二十多年的努力，終於在2010年拿到世界麵包大賽冠軍。回台灣以後，他開了自己的麵包店，一年能做兩億的生意。他的夢想是成為企業家，能在亞洲各國開分店。新加坡大學也因為這個原因錄取了他。吳寶春成功的故事鼓勵了很多人做自己。可是，是不是每個人都能像他一樣，在碰到機會以前做好百分之百的準備？

### English

#### Bread Competition Champion Wu Baochun

In March 2013, Bakery World Cup champion Wu Baochun passed the interview for the EMBA program at the National University of Singapore and prepared to begin his student life in July. Wu had also applied to an EMBA program in Taiwan, but was rejected because he had only graduated from junior high school and his educational background was considered too limited. His plan to attend graduate school in Singapore sparked considerable discussion in Taiwan. Some people thought the schools were too inflexible: times had changed, and the system should change too. Others felt that his academic foundation was insufficient, and that admitting him would be unfair to people who had studied hard.

Wu Baochun was born in the countryside of southern Taiwan and was the youngest child in his family. His family was poor, and his father died when he was young. His mother worked hard every day to raise eight children. As a child, Wu did not understand the importance of education and did not enjoy studying. By his own account, when he graduated from junior high school, he knew fewer than 500 characters.

At seventeen, he left his hometown and went alone to Taipei to apprentice at a bakery. Because he had not studied much, he faced many difficulties at work. That was when he understood the importance of education. During military service, he used his free time to learn characters by watching television. Later, he studied Japanese because he wanted to understand Japanese baking books. A few years later, work took him to Japan to study. The experience opened his eyes: he realized that baking bread was not only a professional skill, but also an art.

After more than twenty years of hard work, he finally won the Bakery World Cup in 2010. After returning to Taiwan, he opened his own bakery, which can bring in NT$200 million in annual business. His dream is to become an entrepreneur and open branches in countries across Asia. The University of Singapore admitted him for this reason. Wu Baochun’s success story has encouraged many people to be themselves. But can everyone, like him, be one hundred percent prepared before an opportunity comes along?

## 文法 Grammar

### I. 不是……就是…… if it’s not…, then it’s…

*(08-05)*

**Function:** Literally, this says that one of two alternatives must be true. It can also exaggerate or complain about a repeated situation.

**Structure:** Subject + 不是 + A，就是 + B。

- 我新買的手機不是收不到訊號，就是自動關機，我非拿去門市退換不可。
- 桌上這件外套我想不是敏萱的，就是雅婷的。
- 放假的時候，他不是上網玩遊戲，就是在家看漫畫、放鬆心情。
- 他整天不是抱怨工作太多，就是抱怨薪水太少，讓人聽得好煩。
- 暑假，他不是去打工，就是回家照顧爺爺，所以沒時間跟我們玩。

**練習 Exercise**

Complete the dialogues using 不是……就是…….

- A：聽說小王要參加公職人員考試。 B：他整天＿＿＿＿，怎麼考得上？
- A：小王怎麼又遲到了？真討厭。 B：你別生氣。我想＿＿＿＿。
- A：這些豬肉餡兒的水餃樣子很特別，都是雅婷包的嗎？ B：不清楚。我想＿＿＿＿。
- A：現在有些年輕人結婚以後不想生孩子，你知道為什麼嗎？ B：我想＿＿＿＿。
- A：為什麼他最近看起來那麼沒有精神？ B：＿＿＿＿。

### II. 算了 forget it; drop it

*(08-06)*

**Function:** 算了 is an idiomatic verb phrase used when the speaker decides to let go of an unpleasant matter.

**Structure:** 算了 can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of a sentence.

- A：你不是要跟女朋友去聽五月天的演唱會嗎？怎麼還在這裡？ B：氣死我了。她說要準備明天的報告，不去了。 A：她不去算了，你不要生氣了。
- A：妳昨天會計的考試，成績怎麼樣？ B：真不好。算了，算了，今天不說這個。
- A：小王說他對唱歌沒興趣，晚上不去KTV了。 B：那就算了，我自己去。
- A：沒想到我最喜歡的羊毛外套破了一個大洞。 B：既然破了一個大洞，就丟了算了。
- A：我告訴小明炸的東西對身體不好，不要常吃，他總是不聽。 B：他不聽算了，你別難過了。

**Usage:** Its flexible position is unusual: compare 算了，你別生氣 / 她不去算了 / 他不聽算了.

**練習 Exercise**

Complete the dialogues using 算了.

- A：氣死了，我孩子又把手機弄壞了。 B：算了，別氣了，＿＿＿＿。
- A：你不是說週末要去海邊曬太陽，怎麼不去了？ B：算了，＿＿＿＿。
- A：雖然念會計系比較容易找到工作，但是我一點興趣都沒有。 B：既然沒興趣，就＿＿＿＿。
- A：去美國念書是難得的機會，妳怎麼不去呢？ B：我是家裡的獨生女，恐怕爸媽捨不得我去那麼遠的地方。 A：要是妳父母捨不得妳去，就＿＿＿＿。
- A：小陳說下星期有考試，所以今天晚上不能來和我們一起過中秋節。 B：＿＿＿＿。

### III. 這樣一來 that being the case; that way

*(08-07)*

**Function:** Introduces a possible consequence of the situation just mentioned.

**Structure:** Situation. 這樣一來，+ consequence.

- A：聽說陳平這個學期有好幾門課被當。 B：這樣一來，他明年恐怕沒辦法畢業了。
- A：我先生下星期要帶孩子去美國旅行一個月。 B：真的啊。這樣一來，妳就不必天天做飯了。
- A：電視新聞說這個週末有颱風要來。 B：這樣一來，我們就不能去海邊玩了。
- A：媽媽建議讓弟弟下課以後去學游泳、書法跟網球。 B：太好了。這樣一來，他就不會整天在家看電視、打電玩了。
- A：最近三個星期，我住的城市幾乎天天下雨，而且新聞還說下個禮拜雨還不會停。 B：這樣一來，你的衣服、鞋子不就都發霉了嗎？

**練習 Exercise**

Respond with a consequence using 這樣一來.

- A：小明的爸爸說，如果他要修輔系，就得自己付學費。 B：＿＿＿＿。
- A：我打算今年暑假參加公職人員考試。 B：＿＿＿＿。
- A：聽說從這個學期起，成功的企業家不必參加筆試，只要通過口試就能上研究所。 B：這怎麼可以呢？這樣一來，＿＿＿＿。
- A：小王已經決定念服裝設計系，不念醫學系了。 B：＿＿＿＿。
- A：爸爸答應我，下個月開始，每個月多給我兩千塊錢生活費。 B：＿＿＿＿。

### IV. 早就……了 long since; already long ago

*(08-08)*

**Function:** Emphasizes that something happened much earlier than the present point of reference—often with a sense of “ages ago.”

**Structure:** Subject + 早就 + verb / state + 了.

- 我只見過陳平幾次面，早就對他沒印象了。
- 五月天演唱會的票，我早就買了，你不必擔心。
- 那條路上早就沒有日本人留下來的木造房子了。我們還要去嗎？
- 你的平板電腦我早就修理好了，你怎麼不記得呢？
- 媽媽煮的水餃，早就被弟弟吃光了。你吃別的吧！

**練習 Exercise**

Complete with 早就……了 to show the event happened long ago.

- A：小李住在台灣一年了，還沒拿到居留證嗎？ B：他＿＿＿＿。
- A：你轉系的手續辦得怎麼樣？ B：沒想到轉系的手續那麼麻煩，我＿＿＿＿。
- A：你不是很喜歡王美美嗎？怎麼不追她？ B：追她的人這麼多，我＿＿＿＿。
- A：你爺爺還在鄉下種水果嗎？ B：小農的利潤那麼低，我爺爺＿＿＿＿。
- A：王明跟他女朋友同居了好幾年了，怎麼不結婚呢？ B：誰說的？他們＿＿＿＿，而且還生了一個孩子。

### V. 從……起 starting from; as of

*(08-09)*

**Function:** Marks the starting point of an event or a continuing situation.

**Structure:** 從 + starting point + 起，+ event/situation. 從……起 is more formal than 從……開始.

- 學生總是說：「從明天起，我要好好地念書，不讓父母失望。」
- 那個語言中心規定從今年秋天起，申請獎學金的學生，成績要有八十五分。
- 因為放暑假的關係，學校的圖書館從後天起，上午九點才開門。
- 你弄錯了。百貨公司的打折活動是從十月十號起，不是十月一號。
- 那個展覽館從下星期一起到下個月三十一號，要舉行電腦展。

**Usage:** In casual speech, 從……開始 is often more natural; 從……起 is relatively formal or written.

**練習 Exercise**

Complete using 從……起.

- A：她是什麼時候開始在餐廳打工的？ B：＿＿＿＿，她就在餐廳打工了。
- A：小王決定去日本念研究所了嗎？ B：是啊，他終於決定＿＿＿＿去日本念三年的書。
- A：你是什麼時候開始學做飯的？ B：＿＿＿＿，就開始學做飯了。
- A：小美網球怎麼打得那麼好？ B：因為她＿＿＿＿，就學打網球了。
- A：玉真，妳這次的口頭報告做得不太好。 B：我知道了。＿＿＿＿，我會認真一點。

### VI. 卻 however; contrary to expectation

*(08-10)*

**Function:** Signals that the second event contrasts with what would normally be expected from the first.

**Structure:** Clause 1，(可是／但是／沒想到) + subject + 卻 + verb phrase.

- 他的成績不錯，可是卻沒通過研究所的面試。
- 在那家麵包店當學徒，雖然辛苦，卻讓他大開眼界。
- 我昨天買的外套裡面破了一個洞，但是店員卻不讓我退換。
- 早上出門的時候還出太陽，沒想到現在卻下起雨來了。
- 那家小吃店雖然沒有招牌，生意卻好得不得了。

**Usage:** 卻 commonly follows 可是、但是 or 沒想到 and usually comes before the verb phrase in the contrasting clause.

**練習 Exercise**

Complete with 卻 to show an unexpected contrast.

- 他想買的鞋子是有名的牌子，可是＿＿＿＿。
- 我以為媽媽已經答應讓我養寵物了，沒想到＿＿＿＿。
- 李明雖然從小在農村長大，＿＿＿＿。
- 他穿的那件藍色外套看起來跟我買的一模一樣，但是＿＿＿＿。
- 美真不但長得美，而且能力強，追她的人很多，＿＿＿＿。

### VII. 因為……而…… because of…, and as a result…

*(08-11)*

**Function:** 而 introduces a result arising from the cause given with 因為. This pattern is common in writing and formal contexts.

**Structure:** Subject + 因為 + cause + 而 + result.

- 我奶奶一個人住，因為怕孤單而養了兩隻狗。
- 很多人因為想學道地的西班牙文而去西班牙。
- 那個小鎮因為今年芒果收成很好而打算舉行慶祝活動。
- 她因為衣服、鞋子都發霉了而決定去買除濕機。
- 王小姐因為男朋友忘了送她生日禮物而氣得不想跟他說話。

**練習 Exercise**

Complete the result clauses with 而.

- 他因為爺爺跌倒而＿＿＿＿。
- 他因為想接近自然而＿＿＿＿。
- 小陳今天因為上課不專心而＿＿＿＿。
- 何真真因為想減輕父母的負擔而打算＿＿＿＿。
- 王老闆因為明美的口才好而＿＿＿＿。

### VIII. 經過 after; following

*(08-12)*

**Function:** Here 經過 is a preposition meaning “after” or “following.” It can also be a verb meaning “to pass through” a place.

**Structure:** 經過 + noun phrase / clause，+ result. (Verb use: 經過 + place.)

- 經過父母多次的說明，她才明白要成功非努力不可。
- 他的喉嚨經過多天的休息，最近好一點了。
- 你並沒有經過學校的同意，怎麼可以使用體育館？
- 我是經過兩年的準備，才考上公務員的。
- 從台北去高雄，要經過台南。

**練習 Exercise**

Complete with 經過 to describe a process or experience and its result.

- A：那件藍色外套不便宜，何雅婷怎麼就買了？ B：＿＿＿＿才買的。（考慮）
- A：這份合約，你都看懂了嗎？ B：＿＿＿＿，我終於懂了。（說明）
- A：你們班畢業旅行要去哪裡？決定了嗎？ B：＿＿＿＿，我們決定去泰國四天。（討論）
- A：小明的太極拳怎麼打得這麼好？ B：＿＿＿＿，才打得這麼好的。（練習）
- A：你是怎麼見到王老闆的？ B：我＿＿＿＿，才見到王老闆的。（陳先生的安排）

## Grammar Examples in English

### I. 不是……就是……

- My new phone either can't get a signal or shuts itself off, so I have to take it to the store to exchange it.
- During vacation, he's either online playing games or at home reading comics to relax.
- He either complains that he has too much work or that his salary is too low all day long.

### II. 算了

- If she isn't going, forget it. Don't get upset.
- Forget it, forget it. Let's not talk about this today.
- If he won't listen, just let it go. Don't be sad.

### III. 這樣一來

- I heard Chen Ping failed several courses this semester. In that case, he probably won't be able to graduate next year.
- A typhoon is coming this weekend. That means we can't go to the beach.

### IV. 早就……了

- I met Chen Ping only a few times; I've long since forgotten what he is like.
- I bought the concert tickets ages ago. You don't need to worry.
- My brother ate all the dumplings Mom made long ago. Have something else.

### V. 從……起

- Starting tomorrow, I'll study hard so I won't disappoint my parents.
- Starting this fall, scholarship applicants must have a grade of at least 85.
- The library will open at nine in the morning starting the day after tomorrow.

### VI. 卻

- His grades were good, but he didn't pass the graduate-school interview.
- It was sunny when I left home this morning, but unexpectedly it's raining now.
- That little restaurant has no sign, yet business is exceptionally good.

### VII. 因為……而……

- My grandmother lives alone; because she is afraid of loneliness, she has two dogs.
- Many people go to Spain because they want to learn authentic Spanish.
- Because her clothes and shoes got moldy, she decided to buy a dehumidifier.

### VIII. 經過

- After her parents explained it several times, she finally understood that success requires hard work.
- His throat is a little better after several days of rest.
- I passed the civil-service exam only after two years of preparation.

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### How Children Are Named

The names Chinese parents choose often reveal their hopes for their children. Names such as 來弟 (*láidì*, “come, little brother”), 引弟 (*yǐndì*, “bring a little brother”), and 招弟 (*zhāodì*, “beckon a little brother”) are traditionally given to girls. The names reflect a preference for sons: parents hoped the next baby would be a boy who could carry on the family line. Some parents considered these names too direct, so they chose 娣 (*dì*), which combines 女 “woman” and 弟 “younger brother,” or 希男 (*xī’nán*, “hope for a boy”). Other names used near-homophones of auspicious phrases—for example, 士蘭 (*shìlán*, “scholar orchid”) sounds somewhat like 是男 (*shì nán*, “it’s a boy”).

Boys, by contrast, were often given auspicious names such as 天賜 (*tiāncì*, “heaven granted”), 萬福 (*wànfú*, “ten thousand blessings”), or 進財 (*jìncái*, “bring treasure”). Taiwanese fortune tellers divide names into auspicious 吉 (*jí*) and inauspicious 凶 (*xiōng*), partly according to the total number of strokes in the family and given names. Some parents believe that the stroke count influences a child's life, so they seek lucky names. This helps explain why many people share similar-sounding names, such as the girls 怡君 (*Yíjūn*) and 雅婷 (*Yǎtíng*) or the boys 志強 (*Zhìqiáng*) and 建宏 (*Jiànhóng*): the names sound pleasant and have positive meanings.

Parents hope their children will succeed. A boy might be named 龍成 (*Lóngchéng*) in the hope that he will 成為龍 (*chéngwéi lóng*, “become a dragon,” meaning become a prominent person). A girl might be named 巧玲 (*Qiǎolíng*) in the hope that she will be intelligent, sensitive, and skilled. The names parents choose express their hopes and affection for their children.
