# 第十二課 怎麼吃才健康？

*Dì shí èr kè — Zěnme chī cái jiànkāng?*

How to Have a Healthy Diet?

> **Note on pinyin:** Textbook 2 prints no romanization — its text blocks are Traditional + Simplified + English only. The pinyin below is supplied editorially so the lesson can be read aloud; it is not printed in the source.

## 學習目標 Learning Objectives

**Topic: 飲食文化 Food Culture**

- Learning to talk about my eating habits and the eating customs in my country.
- Learning to talk about the good and bad aspects of an event or situation.
- Learning to give suggestions on healthy eating.
- Learning to talk about the different flavors of food and how to make food.

## 對話 Dialogue

**月美：** 我正在等白如玉，我們要去吃飯，你想跟我們一起去嗎？

*Yuèměi: Wǒ zhèngzài děng Bái Rúyù, wǒmen yào qù chīfàn, nǐ xiǎng gēn wǒmen yìqǐ qù ma?*

Yuemei: I am waiting for Bai Ruyu. We're going to go eat. Would you like to join us?

**馬丁：** 好啊！妳們要去吃什麼？

*Mǎdīng: Hǎo a! Nǐmen yào qù chī shénme?*

Martin: Sure! What are you going to eat?

**月美：** 我們打算去學校對面一家新開的素食餐廳。

*Yuèměi: Wǒmen dǎsuàn qù xuéxiào duìmiàn yì jiā xīn kāi de sùshí cāntīng.*

Yuemei: We are planning to go to a new vegetarian restaurant across the street from the school.

**馬丁：** 速食？學校對面有「麥當勞」？我怎麼沒注意到？

*Mǎdīng: Sùshí? Xuéxiào duìmiàn yǒu "Màidāngláo"? Wǒ zěnme méi zhùyì dào?*

Martin: Fast food? There's a McDonald's across the street from school? How come I never noticed?

**月美：** 你想錯了，不是麥當勞那種速食店，是不吃肉的素食餐廳。

*Yuèměi: Nǐ xiǎng cuò le, bú shì Màidāngláo nà zhǒng sùshí diàn, shì bù chī ròu de sùshí cāntīng.*

Yuemei: You're mistaken. Not fast food like McDonald's. It's vegetarian. The kind that doesn't have any meat dishes.

**馬丁：** 新開的餐廳我都想去嚐一嚐，好，我跟妳們一起去。

*Mǎdīng: Xīn kāi de cāntīng wǒ dōu xiǎng qù cháng yi cháng, hǎo, wǒ gēn nǐmen yìqǐ qù.*

Martin: I like to try out all new restaurants. Sure, I'll go with you two.

（白如玉到了）

*(Bái Rúyù dào le)*

(Ruyu is here.)

**如玉：** 欸！馬丁，好久不見！月美，對不起，讓妳等了這麼久。

*Rúyù: Èi! Mǎdīng, hǎojiǔ bú jiàn! Yuèměi, duìbuqǐ, ràng nǐ děng le zhème jiǔ.*

Ruyu: Hey! Martin, long time no see! Yuemei, sorry to keep you waiting so long.

**月美：** 沒關係，我也剛下課。馬丁也跟我們去吃飯。

*Yuèměi: Méi guānxi, wǒ yě gāng xiàkè. Mǎdīng yě gēn wǒmen qù chīfàn.*

Yuemei: That's all right. I just got out of class also. Martin is going to join us for lunch.

（餐廳裡）

*(Cāntīng lǐ)*

(In the restaurant)

**如玉：** 馬丁，你拿了這麼多菜，我沒想到你也喜歡吃素。

*Rúyù: Mǎdīng, nǐ ná le zhème duō cài, wǒ méi xiǎngdào nǐ yě xǐhuān chīsù.*

Ruyu: Martin, you got so much food. I didn't know you like to eat vegetarian, also.

**馬丁：** 我是到了台灣以後才覺得吃素不錯，我以前吃漢堡的機會比較多。

*Mǎdīng: Wǒ shì dào le Táiwān yǐhòu cái juéde chīsù búcuò, wǒ yǐqián chī hànbǎo de jīhuì bǐjiào duō.*

Martin: It was only after I arrived in Taiwan that I found that eating vegetarian food is all right. In the past, I had more opportunities to eat hamburgers. (i.e., I didn't have as many opportunities to eat vegetarian food before. Or I used to eat burgers a lot.)

**如玉：** 漢堡吃多了對健康不好，而且容易讓你變胖。

*Rúyù: Hànbǎo chī duō le duì jiànkāng bù hǎo, érqiě róngyì ràng nǐ biàn pàng.*

Ruyu: Eating too many hamburgers is not good for you and it can make you get fat easily.

**月美：** 我在越南就常常吃素。還有，這家餐廳的素菜看起來不油膩，好像每一樣都值得試試。

*Yuèměi: Wǒ zài Yuènán jiù chángcháng chīsù. Háiyǒu, zhè jiā cāntīng de sùcài kàn qǐlái bù yóunì, hǎoxiàng měi yí yàng dōu zhíde shìshi.*

Yuemei: I eat vegetarian food a lot in Vietnam. Also, the food here doesn't look oily. Everything looks worth trying, i.e., so tempting.

**馬丁：** 我很好奇台灣女孩是不是都喜歡吃青菜？她們看起來個個都很瘦。

*Mǎdīng: Wǒ hěn hàoqí Táiwān nǚhái shì bu shì dōu xǐhuān chī qīngcài? Tāmen kàn qǐlái gègè dōu hěn shòu.*

Martin: I'm curious, do all Taiwanese girls like to eat vegetables? Each of them looks very thin.

**月美：** 這也不一定，我室友吃素，但是有一點胖。如玉，我記得妳男朋友不吃肉，對不對？

*Yuèměi: Zhè yě bù yídìng, wǒ shìyǒu chīsù, dànshì yǒu yìdiǎn pàng. Rúyù, wǒ jìde nǐ nánpéngyǒu bù chī ròu, duì bu duì?*

Yuemei: Not necessarily. My roommate is a vegetarian, but also a little heavy. Ruyu, I recall that your boyfriend doesn't eat meat, right?

**如玉：** 對，牛肉、豬肉，他不常吃，他只吃魚。

*Rúyù: Duì, niúròu, zhūròu, tā bù cháng chī, tā zhǐ chī yú.*

Ruyu: Right, he doesn't eat beef or pork often. He only eats fish.

**月美：** 我聽說西方一些吃素的人跟我們不太一樣，我們是完全不吃肉的。

*Yuèměi: Wǒ tīngshuō xīfāng yìxiē chīsù de rén gēn wǒmen bú tài yíyàng, wǒmen shì wánquán bù chī ròu de.*

Yuemei: I hear that in the West, some vegetarians are a bit different from us. We eat no meat at all.

**馬丁：** 我覺得吃素應該是為了健康。

*Mǎdīng: Wǒ juéde chīsù yīnggāi shì wèile jiànkāng.*

Martin: I feel that eating vegetarian food should be done for your health.

**如玉：** 菜的味道不要太鹹，不要常吃炸的、烤的東西，就比較健康。

*Rúyù: Cài de wèidào bú yào tài xián, bú yào cháng chī zhá de, kǎo de dōngxi, jiù bǐjiào jiànkāng.*

Ruyu: It is healthier if your food is not too salty and don't frequently eat things that are deep fried or grilled.

**月美：** 下次有機會，請你們吃我做的越南素食。

*Yuèměi: Xià cì yǒu jīhuì, qǐng nǐmen chī wǒ zuò de Yuènán sùshí.*

Yuemei: Next time when we have the opportunity, I'll treat you to some Vietnamese vegetarian food made by me.

**如玉：** 我也可以做一些青菜沙拉請大家吃。

*Rúyù: Wǒ yě kěyǐ zuò yìxiē qīngcài shālā qǐng dàjiā chī.*

Ruyu: I can make a vegetable salad for everybody.

**馬丁：** 真的？太好了，我希望多吃素，瘦一點，讓自己更健康。

*Mǎdīng: Zhēn de? Tài hǎo le, wǒ xīwàng duō chīsù, shòu yìdiǎn, ràng zìjǐ gèng jiànkāng.*

Martin: Really? That would be great. I want to eat more vegetarian food, lose some weight, and be healthier.

**如玉：** 快一點吃吧！我跟月美等一下還要準備下午的考試。

*Rúyù: Kuài yìdiǎn chī ba! Wǒ gēn Yuèměi děng yíxià hái yào zhǔnbèi xiàwǔ de kǎoshì.*

Ruyu: Hurry up and eat. In a bit, Yuemei and I have an exam to prepare for this afternoon.

白如玉、馬丁和陳月美一起到一家素食餐廳吃午餐，他們聊起吃素的經驗。

他們注意到只要在學校附近就找得到素食餐廳，他們也覺得很多台灣人都不討厭吃素，但是不一定餐餐都吃素；還有，很多台灣人吃素是因為宗教的關係。白如玉說她吃素跟宗教沒有關係，只是覺得吃太多肉容易變胖。她建議馬丁應該少吃一點漢堡、薯條什麼的，多吃青菜，可以讓身體更健康。

馬丁聽了以後說，他可以趁在台灣的機會，多吃吃素食，讓自己瘦下來。

## 文法 Grammar

### I. 讓 ràng — to let someone do something

**Function:** The verb 讓 has several different but related meanings, depending on the sentence and on the context, e.g.,

- 租房子的事，請你讓我想一想。 (let)
- 天氣這麼冷，冷得讓我感冒了。 (make, cause)
- 讓小孩一個人去旅行，不太安全吧！ (permit)
- 老闆讓我做這份工作。 (permit)
- 老師說我說中文說得很流利，讓我很高興。 (make)
- 吃太辣讓妹妹的喉嚨不舒服。 (make)
- 這次旅行讓我更了解台東這個地方了。 (make)

**Structures:**

*Negation:*

- 我跟小王借錢的事，我不讓他告訴別人。
- 颱風來了，媽媽不讓我們出去踢足球。
- 高美玲不讓李東健在泡菜裡放太多辣椒。

*Questions:* Either A-not-A or 是不是 can be used.

- 老闆讓不讓你把公司的車開回家去？
- 妳媽媽讓不讓妳跟我去潛水？
- 你女朋友是不是讓你踢完足球再去找她？

### II. 快一點 kuài yìdiǎn — hurry up

**Function:** This pattern is used in urging the addressee to hurry up with whatever the latter is being engaged in.

- 電影快開始了。我們快一點！
- 哥哥跟弟弟說：「不要上網了，快一點去睡覺！」
- 你最好快一點決定，這個房間可能很快就有人租了。

**Structures:**

*Negation:* Only 不 can negate '快一點 (V)'. The negated pattern indicates a condition, 'If you do not hurry up and…', with conditional conjunctions omitted.

- 你不快一點吃完，我們就不能看電影了。
- 我們不快一點去吃飯，那家店可能就休息了。
- 你不快一點來嚐嚐這個甜點，就被大家吃光了。

**Usage:** '快一點＋V' and 'V＋快一點' are two unrelated constructions. The first 快 refers to time (Hurry up!), while the second 快 refers to manner (fast vs. slowly).

- 快一點跑！　　跑快一點！
- 慢一點去！　　走慢一點！

### III. Verb Particle 起 qǐ — to touch upon

**Function:** When 起 combines with action verbs as their verb-particle, it refers to a meaning of 'to touch upon', e.g., 聊起 'to have talked about', 想起 'to have recalled, to have remembered', 談起 'to have talked about', 說起 'to have spoken of'.

- 我跟朋友一說起台灣小吃，就很有興趣。
- 陳月美一個人在台灣，她想起越南的家人時，有點難過。
- 李東健跟朋友談起找房子的事，朋友告訴他可以上網找。

**Structures:**

*Negation:*

- 老闆在辦公室的時候，別說起薪水的事。
- 他沒跟我談起結婚的事，所以我不能給他什麼建議。
- 我以為他會告訴我旅行的事，可是今天他來家裡都沒聊起。

*Questions:*

- 中午的時候，她們是不是聊起昨天看的電影了？
- 李明華問父母：「你們是不是談起我女朋友了？」
- 我忘了我們是怎麼談起吃素的了？

### IV. 只要 zhǐyào … 就 jiù … — as long as

**Function:** 只要 is a conjunction presenting a condition in the first clause, which is followed by a second clause of consequence, introduced by an adverb 就. As long as the condition is met, the consequence follows.

- 只要坐捷運，就能到台北很多地方去玩。
- 你只要到郵局或是便利商店，就找得到提款機，可以提錢。
- 只要你在說中文的環境裡，你的中文就會進步得快一點。
- 你只要去參觀故宮博物院，就可以看見很多中國古代的東西。
- 你只要多喝水、多休息，感冒很快就會好了。

**Structures:** 只要 is a conjunction, which can appear before or after the subject. 就 is an adverb, and can only appear before a VP.

*Questions:* Only 是不是 can be used.

- 是不是只要他答應跟妳結婚，妳就願意留在台灣？
- 你是不是只要到一個新環境，就很容易感冒？
- 是不是只要房東同意房租少一點，你就不搬家了？

### V. 越 yuè … 越 yuè … — the more…, the more…

**Function:** This pattern expresses that when A is true, B is also true.

- 學生租房子，離學校越近越方便。
- 我聽說辣椒越紅越辣，是不是？
- 很多台灣人說臭豆腐越臭越好吃。

**Structures:** In this pattern, A can be an action or a state, but B can only be a state.

- 很多東西都是用得越久越容易壞。
- 他忘了把資料存在哪裡了，越急越找不到。
- 他們越走越遠，已經不知道回民宿的路了。
- 弟弟現在包餃子，越包越好了。
- 這個音樂，我越聽越喜歡。

*Questions:* 是不是 is the only structure possible for this pattern.

- 你的字是不是寫得越快越不好看？
- 越健康的食物是不是越不好吃？
- 禮物是不是越貴，大家越喜歡？

**Usage:** Both A and B can appear in the same clause, but sometimes they appear in two separate clauses.

- 你越忙，越應該找時間運動運動。
- 泡菜放的時間越長，味道越酸。
- 離捷運站越近的房子，房租越貴。
- 垃圾分類做得越好，可以回收的資源越多。

### VI. Topic as Contrastive

**Function:** A topic in Chinese can serve as the contrastive element in a sentence, i.e., "this, but not that…". Either a subject or an object can be so contrasted.

- A：台灣小吃，你都喜歡嗎？
  B：水餃、包子，我喜歡；臭豆腐，我討厭。
- A：你建議我們帶什麼東西去旅行？
  B：錢、手機一定要帶；藥，不一定要帶。
- A：高美玲有教語言的經驗嗎？
  B：教法文的經驗，她有；教西班牙語的經驗，她沒有。
- A：馬安同是不是會騎車？
  B：機車，他不會騎，他只會騎腳踏車。
- A：他們都會包小籠包嗎？
  B：包小籠包，陳月美不會，可是白如玉會。

### I. 讓 ràng — to let someone do something

**Function:**

- About the renting of the house, please let me think about it.
- The weather is so cold that it caused me to have a cold. (i.e., It's so cold that I caught a cold.)
- Allowing children to travel alone is a bit unsafe, I should think. (i.e., Isn't it a bit unsafe to let children travel alone?)
- The boss is letting me do this work.
- The teacher said that I speak Chinese fluently making me feel happy.
- Your younger sister will get a sore throat if she eats too much spicy food.

**Structures:**

*Negation:*

- I didn't let him tell anyone that I borrowed money from Xiao Wang.
- The typhoon was coming and mom didn't let us go out and play soccer.
- Gao Meiling didn't let Li Dongjian put too many peppers in the kimchi.

*Questions:*

- Does the boss allow you to drive the company car back home?
- Will your mom let you go scuba diving with me?

### II. 快一點 kuài yìdiǎn — hurry up

**Function:**

- The movie is about to start. Let's hurry up.
- The older brother said to the younger brother: "Get off the internet and hurry to bed!"
- You'd better hurry up and decide. This room could be rented by someone very quickly. (i.e., Somebody might take this room any time.)

**Structures:**

*Negation:*

- If you don't hurry up and finish eating, we won't be able to see the movie.
- If we don't hurry up and go eat, the restaurant might be closed (by the time we get there).
- If you don't hurry up and try this dessert, it'll get eaten up by everybody else.

**Usage:**

- Hurry up and run.
- Run faster!
- Take your time and go later!
- Walk slower!

### III. Verb Particle 起 qǐ — to touch upon

**Function:**

- As soon as my friend and I started talking about Taiwanese goodies, i.e., light repasts, our interest piqued.
- Chen Yumei lives in Taiwan alone. Whenever she thinks of her family in Vietnam, she feels a little sad.
- Li Dongjian brought up with his friend the topic of renting an apartment and his friend told him he could search online.

**Structures:**

*Negation:*

- Don't bring up the issue of salary while the boss is in the office.
- He didn't bring up the issue of marriage, so I was unable to give him any advice.
- I thought he was going to tell me about his travels, but when he came to my house today, he didn't bring it up.

*Questions:*

- At noon did they chat about the movie they saw yesterday?
- Li Minghua asked his parents, "Are you guys talking about my girlfriend?"
- I forget how we got started talking about vegetarianism (or eating vegetarian food). (i.e., I forget how the topic of vegetarians/vegetarian food came up.)

### IV. 只要 zhǐyào … 就 jiù … — as long as

**Function:**

- As long as, i.e., if, you take the MRT, you can get to many places in Taipei.
- As long as, i.e., if, you go to a post office or a convenience store, you can find an ATM to withdraw money.
- As long as you are in a Chinese-speaking environment, your Chinese will improve a bit more quickly.
- If you visit the Palace Museum, you will see many things from ancient China.
- As long as, i.e., if, you drink a lot of water and rest a lot, your cold will be OK very quickly. (i.e., Drink plenty of fluids and get plenty of rest and you'll be over your cold very quickly.)

**Structures:**

*Questions:*

- As long as he agrees to marry you, you would be willing to stay in Taiwan, right?
- If you go to a new environment, you catch cold easily, right? (i.e., Whenever you change environments or go to a new place, you catch cold easily, right?)
- As long as the landlord agrees to lower the rent a bit, you won't move out, right?

### V. 越 yuè … 越 yuè … — the more…, the more…

**Function:**

- For a student renting an apartment, the closer it is to school, the more convenient it is.
- I heard that the redder a pepper is, the hotter it is. Is that true?
- Many Taiwanese say that, for stinky tofu, the stinkier it is, the better.

**Structures:**

- For a lot of things, the longer you use them, the easier, i.e., more likely, it is that they'll break.
- He forgot where he saved the information, and the more anxious he got, the more difficult it became to find.
- They kept walking further and further away until they had no idea how to get back to the B&B.
- The dumplings wrapped by my little brother are being wrapped better and better. (i.e., My little brother is getting better and better at wrapping dumplings.)
- The more I listen to this music, the more I like it.

*Questions:*

- The faster you write, the uglier your characters become, right?
- The healthier the food is, the worse it tastes, right?
- The more expensive a gift is, the better it is received, right?

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### Vegetarianism in Taiwan

*[printed in English in the textbook]*

Because of Buddhism's profound influence on Taiwanese culture, many Taiwanese are vegetarians. Others, following traditions dating back to the island's agrarian past, refrain from eating beef, though they do eat other types of meat. However, because of economic and social changes that have come about over the years, fewer and fewer people eat all three daily meals at home or cook for themselves. Nowadays, most people eat out a good deal of the time. As a result, the number of vegetarians has decreased. Unless someone is a truly devout Buddhist, he or she is likely to settle for the next best thing—not eating meat on the 1st and 15th of every lunar month.

In addition, those who practice vegetarianism for religious reasons have also made concessions—as long as they do not partake of actual animal flesh, they can enjoy eggs and dairy products, as well as dishes that include those ingredients. In Taiwan, many foods are labeled "completely vegetarian," "lacto-vegetarian" or "lacto-ovo-vegetarian." Buddhist monks and nuns in Taiwan don't eat eggs, but can consume milk, cheese, and other dairy products.
