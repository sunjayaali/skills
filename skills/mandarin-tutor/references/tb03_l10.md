# 第十課 我住院了

*Dì shí kè — Wǒ zhùyuàn le*

*I\'m Staying in the Hospital*

> **Pinyin note:** Book 3 prints zhuyin, not pinyin. Pinyin is editorial; vocabulary readings follow the official lesson deck. The zhuyin in the scan is not reliably legible.

## 學習目標 Learning Objectives

**Topic: 醫療 Health Care**

- Learning to describe what happened in an automobile accident.
- Learning to explain injuries and discuss treatment with doctors.
- Learning to discuss issues related to cosmetic surgery.
- Learning to provide data and real-life examples supporting your views.

## 對話 Dialogue

*(10-01)*

**羅珊蒂：** 怎麼回事？你的手怎麼了？臉也受傷了。

*Luó Shāndì: Zěnme le shì? Nǐ de shǒu zěnme le? Liǎn yě shòushāng le.*

**李文彥：** 我出車禍了。我上個禮拜騎腳踏車來學校的路上被一輛車撞了。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Wǒ chū chēhuò le. Wǒ shàng ge lǐbài qí jiǎotà chē lái xuéxiào de lù shàng bèi yí liàng chē zhuàng le.*

**羅珊蒂：** 你一定是為了趕時間，不遵守交通規則。

*Luó Shāndì: Nǐ yídìng shì wèile gǎn shíjiān, bù zūnshǒu jiāotōng guīzé.*

**李文彥：** 哪裡是我的錯。我過馬路的時候，有一輛右轉車不讓我，把我撞倒了。我躺在地上，痛得起不來。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Nǎlǐ shì wǒ de cuò. Wǒ guò mǎlù de shíhou, yǒu yí liàng yòu zhuǎn chē bù ràng wǒ, bǎ wǒ zhuàng dǎo le. Wǒ tǎng zài dìshàng, tòng de qǐ bù lái.*

**羅珊蒂：** 好可怕！當時有沒有人幫你？誰送你去醫院的？

*Luó Shāndì: Hǎo kěpà! Dāngshí yǒu méiyǒu rén bāng nǐ? Shuí sòng nǐ qù yīyuàn de?*

**李文彥：** 撞到我的人打電話報警。救護車馬上把我送到急診室去了。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Zhuàng dào wǒ de rén dǎ diànhuà bàojǐng. Jiùhù chē mǎshàng bǎ wǒ sòng dào jízhěnshì qù le.*

**陳敏萱：** 你的傷嚴重嗎？(看著李文彥的手) 是不是骨折了？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Nǐ de shāng yánzhòng ma? (Kàn zhe Lǐ Wényàn de shǒu) Shìbushì gǔzhé le?*

**李文彥：** 是啊，我一進急診室，醫生就讓我去照X光。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Shì a, wǒ yī jìn jízhěnshì, yīshēng jiù ràng wǒ qù zhào X guāng.*

**陳敏萱：** 後來呢？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Hòulái ne?*

**李文彥：** 醫生看了片子，要我住院開刀。我住院住了五天呢。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Yīshēng kàn le piànzǐ, yào wǒ zhùyuàn kāidāo. Wǒ zhùyuàn zhùle wǔ tiān ne.*

**陳敏萱：** 我聽說大醫院的急診室總是擠滿了病人。如果情況不是那麼緊急的話，有時候要等幾天才等得到病床，真的嗎？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Wǒ tīngshuō dà yīyuàn de jízhěnshì zǒngshì jǐ mǎnle bìngrén. Rúguǒ qíngkuàng bù shì nàme jǐnjí de huà, yǒu shíhòu yào děng jǐ tiān cái děngdào bìngchuáng, zhēnde ma?*

**李文彥：** 是啊，我那個時候痛得要命，醫生先給我打止痛針。可是他說還有更緊急的病人，讓我等一等。最快到第二天才能動手術。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Shì a, wǒ nàge shíhou tòng de yàomìng, yīshēng xiān gěi wǒ dǎ zhǐtòng zhēn. Kěshì tā shuō hái yǒu gèng jǐnjí de bìngrén, ràng wǒ děng yí děng. Zuì kuài dào dì èr tiān cái néng dòng shǒushù.*

**陳敏萱：** 出車禍的事，你家人知道嗎？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Chū chēhuò de shì, nǐ jiārén zhīdào ma?*

**李文彥：** 警察幫我打電話給我阿姨。她一聽說要開刀，緊張得一直問醫生，有沒有危險，以後動作會不會受到影響。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Jǐngchá bāng wǒ dǎ diànhuà gěi wǒ āyí. Tā yī tīngshuō yào kāidāo, jǐnzhāng de yīzhí wèn yīshēng, yǒu méiyǒu wéixiǎn, yǐhòu dòngzuò huì buhuì shòudào yǐngxiǎng.*

**羅珊蒂：** 醫生怎麼說？

*Luó Shāndì: Yīshēng zěnme shuō?*

**李文彥：** 醫生說這不算大手術，可是開刀多少會有一些風險。至於動作會不會受到影響，得看受傷的部位。最好的情況大概可以恢復八成到九成。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Yīshēng shuō zhè búsuàn dà shǒushù, kěshì kāidāo duōshǎo huì yǒu yīxiē fēngxiǎn. Zhìyú dòngzuò huì buhuì shòudào yǐngxiǎng, děi kàn shòushāng de bùwèi. Zuì hǎo de qíngkuàng dàgài kěyǐ huīfù bā chéng dào jiǔ chéng.*

**羅珊蒂：** 你現在覺得怎麼樣了？

*Luó Shāndì: Nǐ xiànzai juéde zěnme yàng le?*

**李文彥：** 我還好，不怎麼痛了。可是很不方便，連穿衣服都要別人幫忙。只好搬到阿姨家去住一段時間。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Wǒ hái hǎo, bú zěnme tòng le. Kěshì hěn bù fāngbiàn, lián chuān yīfu dōu yào biérén bāngmáng. Zhǐ hǎo bān dào āyí jiā qù zhù yí duàn shíjiān.*

**陳敏萱：** 你在阿姨家還習慣嗎？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Nǐ zài āyí jiā hái xíguàn ma?*

**李文彥：** 我跟阿姨很親，她像媽媽一樣地照顧我，每天都煮魚湯給我喝。你看我現在除了手不太方便以外，不是都很好嗎？

*Lǐ Wényàn: Wǒ gēn āyí hěn qīn, tā xiàng māma yàng de zhàogù wǒ, měitiān dōu zhǔ yútāng gěi wǒ hē. Nǐ kàn wǒ xiànzai chúle shǒu bù tài fāngbiàn yǐwài, búshì dōu hǎo ma?*

**陳敏萱：** 喝魚湯？你阿姨為什麼讓你每天喝魚湯？

*Chén Mǐnxuān: Hē yútāng? Nǐ āyí wèishénme ràng nǐ měitiān hē yútāng?*

**李文彥：** 阿姨說多喝魚湯，傷口會恢復得比較快。哎呀，我應該要走了。我得去看醫生。明天見。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Āyí shuō duō hē yútāng, shāngkǒu huì huīfù de bǐjiào kuài. Āiya, wǒ yīnggāi yào zǒu le. Wǒ děi qù kàn yīshēng. Míngtiān jiàn.*

### English

**Luo Shandi:** What happened? What happened to your hand? Your face is also injured.

**Li Wenyan:** It was a car accident. Last week, when I was riding my bicycle to school, I was hit by a car on the way.

**Luo Shandi:** You must have been in a hurry and didn\'t follow traffic rules.

**Li Wenyan:** It wasn\'t my fault! When I was crossing the street, a right-turning car didn\'t yield to me and knocked me down. I lay on the ground in so much pain I couldn\'t get up.

**Luo Shandi:** That\'s terrible! Did anyone help you? Who took you to the hospital?

**Li Wenyan:** The person who hit me called the police. An ambulance took me to the ER right away.

**Chen Minxuan:** Were you hurt badly? (Looking at Li Wenyan\'s hand) Is it broken?

**Li Wenyan:** Yeah, as soon as I got to the ER, the doctor sent me for X-rays.

**Chen Minxuan:** And then?

**Li Wenyan:** After looking at the X-ray, the doctor wanted me to stay in the hospital for surgery. I was hospitalized for five days.

**Chen Minxuan:** I\'ve heard ERs at big hospitals are always packed with patients. If it\'s not that urgent, sometimes you have to wait days for a bed — is that true?

**Li Wenyan:** Yeah, I was in terrible pain at the time. The doctor gave me a pain shot first. But he said there were more urgent patients, so he made me wait. The earliest he could do surgery was the next day.

**Chen Minxuan:** Does your family know about the accident?

**Li Wenyan:** The police called my aunt for me. As soon as she heard I needed surgery, she got so nervous she kept asking the doctor if it was dangerous, whether my movement would be affected.

**Luo Shandi:** What did the doctor say?

**Li Wenyan:** The doctor said it\'s not a major surgery, but surgery always carries some risk. As to whether my movement would be affected, that depends on where the injury is. Best case, I\'ll recover 80 to 90 percent.

**Luo Shandi:** How do you feel now?

**Li Wenyan:** I\'m okay. Not much pain anymore. But it\'s very inconvenient — I need help even to get dressed. I had no choice but to move in with my aunt for a while.

**Chen Minxuan:** Are you comfortable living with your aunt?

**Li Wenyan:** I\'m close to my aunt. She takes care of me like a mom. Every day she cooks fish soup for me. Look, except that my hand\'s not fully usable, everything else is fine, right?

**Chen Minxuan:** Fish soup? Why does your aunt make you drink fish soup every day?

**Li Wenyan:** My aunt says lots of fish soup helps the wound heal faster. Yikes, I gotta go. I need to see the doctor. See you tomorrow.

## 閱讀 Reading

### 醫病還是醫美

*(10-02)*

**Traditional:**

何雅婷的香港朋友愛林聽說台灣的微整型技術在亞洲是數一數二的，而且價錢又合理，因此她趁休假到台灣來做微整型。有許多像愛林這樣的外國人利用假期來台灣做醫美手術。他們希望假期結束以後，能變得更迷人，在職場上更有競爭力。

根據調查，百分之六十一的人認為整型除了可以增加自信，還可以改善職場的人際關係。另外，研究也發現，外表好看的人收入比外表普通的人多百分之十到十五。因為這樣，這幾年醫美成為很大的商機，一年有好幾百億的產值，而且一直在增加。也因此，全台灣44,000多位醫生，其中有12,000多位從事醫美。

醫生的工作是醫治病人，社會地位一向很高，但是現在為什麼有這麼多的醫生選擇去做一般人認為不需要太多專業技術的微整型？主要原因有兩個：一個是在一般的醫院工作辛勞，待遇又不高，另外一個是社會環境改變了。

就拿公立醫院外科醫生來說，每星期工作時間超過一百個小時但是薪水只有十萬塊。可是當微整型醫生，每星期只要工作四十五個小時，薪水卻是兩、三倍。另一方面是價值觀的問題。以前醫生受人尊敬，可是現在的病人動不動就告醫生；平均每天最少有一位醫生被告；醫療糾紛也給醫生帶來很大的壓力。

很多人擔心，我們的醫療品質會不會因為那麼多外科醫生去從事整型工作而受到影響？可是如果醫生的工作環境和待遇不能改善，我們怎麼能怪他們選擇醫美。再說，醫美也是幫助人找回自信，開始新生活的好方法，不是嗎？

**Simplified:**

何雅婷的香港朋友爱林听说台湾的微整形技术在亚洲是数一数二的，而且价钱又合理，因此她趁休假到台湾来做微整形。有许多像爱林这样的外国人利用假期来台湾做医美手术。他们希望假期结束以后，能变得更迷人，在职场上更有竞争力。

根据调查，百分之六十一的人认为整形除了可以增加自信，还可以改善职场的人际关系。另外，研究也发现，外表好看的人收入比外表普通的人多百分之十到十五。因为这样，这几年医美成为很大的商机，一年有好几百亿的产值，而且一直在增加。也因此，全台湾44,000多位医生，其中有12,000多位从事医美。

医生的工作是医治病人，社会地位一向很高，但是现在为什么有这么多的医生选择去做一般人认为不需要太多专业技术的微整形？主要原因有两个：一个是在一般的医院工作辛劳，待遇又不高，另外一个是社会环境改变了。

就拿公立医院外科医生来说，每星期工作时间超过一百个小时但是薪水只有十万块。可是当微整形医生，每星期只要工作四十五个小时，薪水却是两、三倍。另一方面是价值观的问题。以前医生受人尊敬，可是现在的病人动不动就告医生；平均每天最少有一位医生被告；医疗纠纷也给医生带来很大的压力。

很多人担心，我们的医疗品质会不会因为那么多外科医生去从事整形工作而受到影響？可是如果医生的工作环境和待遇不能改善，我们怎么能怪他们选择医美。再说，医美也是帮助人找回自信，开始新生活的好方法，不是嗎？

**English:**

He Yating\'s friend from Hong Kong, Ailin, heard that cosmetic surgery skills in Taiwan are among the best in Asia, and the price is reasonable too, so she took advantage of her vacation to come to Taiwan and have plastic surgery done. There are many foreigners like Ailin who use their vacations to come to Taiwan for cosmetic surgery. They hope that by the time their vacation is over, they will be more attractive and more competitive in the workplace.

According to surveys, 61% of people believe that apart from increasing self-confidence, cosmetic surgery can improve interpersonal relations in the workplace. Studies have also found that attractive people earn 10% to 15% more than people with average looks. Because of this, in recent years cosmetic surgery has become a big business worth hundreds of billions of New Taiwan dollars a year, and it keeps growing. As a result, of Taiwan\'s more than 44,000 doctors, over 12,000 are engaged in cosmetic surgery.

A doctor\'s job is to heal the sick, and their social status has always been high. But why are so many doctors now choosing to go into cosmetic surgery, which is generally considered not to require much specialized skill? There are two main reasons. First, the work is exhausting at a general hospital, and the pay isn\'t high. Second, the social landscape has changed.

Take surgeons at public hospitals, for example. They work more than 100 hours a week, but their salaries are only NT$100,000 a month. But as a plastic surgeon, you work 45 hours a week and your salary can be two to three times that amount. Then there\'s the issue of values. In the past, doctors were respected, but now patients sue doctors at the drop of a hat — at least one doctor is sued every day on average. Medical disputes also give doctors a great deal of stress.

Many people worry whether our medical quality will be affected because so many surgeons are going into plastic surgery. But if doctors\' working conditions and pay can\'t be improved, how can we blame them for choosing cosmetic surgery? Besides, cosmetic surgery is also a good way to help people rediscover their self-confidence and start a new life, isn\'t it?

## 文法 Grammar

### I. 至於 — introducing a new topic

*(10-05)*

**Function:** 至於 is a new-topic marker, introducing or shifting to a new topic that is related to an announcement mentioned in a previous sentence.

**Structures:** 至於 + NP / clause

**Usage:**

- 聽說李文彥受傷了。至於傷得怎麼樣，我就不清楚了。
- 這件外套的大小、樣子都很合適。至於顏色，我覺得淺了一點。
- 我對醫學系完全沒興趣，我想當警察。至於我父母，他們當然反對。我也沒辦法，我要做自己。
- 我吃東西只講究營養。至於味道好不好，沒關係。
- 她喜歡個性好的人。至於外表，她覺得不重要。

### II. 看 — presenting a determining factor

**Function:** The verb 看 has many meanings. In this lesson, it presents a factor, its object, that determines an issue.

**Structures:** 得 + Verb + 看 + Noun (the determining factor)

**Usage:** All the sentences above have a "whether... or not" segment, but this is not a redundancy.

- 考不考得上公職，除了努力以外，還得看運氣。
- 傷口多久會好，得看是哪裡受傷。

### III. 一向 — habitual/always

*(10-07)*

**Function:** The adverb 一向 means "it has always been the case that", referring to habits or aptitudes.

**Structures:** Subject + 一向 + Adj/Verb

**Usage:**

1. Both 一向 and 一直 indicate the continuation of an action or a state from the past to the present. 一向, however stresses something habitual, while 一直 emphasizes continuation. They are thus not always interchangeable.
   - (從昨天到今天,)他一直不相信我說的話。
   - 他一向不相信我說的話。
   - 她一向不聽別人的建議，你別再說了。(一直)
   - 他旅行以前，一向先計畫好。(一直)
2. Although 一向 indicates habit, the habit can change.
   - 這裡的冬天一向很潮濕，今年卻變了。
   - 他一向不喜歡吃海鮮，昨天卻點了一道檸魚。
3. 一向 can only be used to describe something in the past, while 一直 can be used to express something in the future.
   - 他會一直陪著你走到學校。(一向)

### V. Passive Sentences with 受

*(10-09)*

**Function:** Sentences with the transitive verb 受 indicate a passive meaning, "to be...", "to get...".

**Structures:**

1. 受 + O (object)
   - 她受過很好的教育 (jiàoyù, education)。
   - 他選擇放棄，應該是受了很大的壓力。
2. 受 + S + V
   - 他教書很活潑，很受學生(的)歡迎。
   - 她寫的歌很受大家(的)喜愛。
   - 他提出的想法很受店長(的)重視。

**Usage:**
A. Comparing the passive with 受 and the passive with 被.

1. 被 is a passive particle, and 受 is a verb. Words like 很, 非常, and 大 can be placed in front of 受 as adverbials. These cannot be placed in front of 被.
   - 比賽的時候天氣不好，他的成績大受影響。
   - 聽了吳寶春的故事，他很受感動。
   - 他把學生當做孩子，非常受學生尊敬。
2. 被 — passives relate to events that are often unfortunate for the subject or the speaker, whereas 受 — passives are free from this tendency.
   - 她的手機被朋友壞了。
   - 這件事情被她發現就麻煩了。
   - 他偷東西的時候被人看見了。
   - 她很喜歡這個電影，看了以後很受感動。
   - 我喜歡接近自然，是受她影響。
   - 她念書很專心，不受別人打擾。
3. 受 is in most cases used with transitive state verbs. 被, on the other hand, is in most cases used with action verbs.
   - 他把賣菜賺的錢都給了竊人，很受人尊敬。
   - 他說話很有意思，很受朋友歡迎。
   - 這種遊戲很有意思，很受高中學生喜愛。
   - 他沒把事情做好，被老闆罵了一頓。
   - 我的車上個週末停在路邊，結果被撞壞了。倒楣死了！
   - 他安靜地走進辦公室，不想被發現。

## Grammar Examples in English

### I. 至於 — Introducing a New Topic

**Function:**

- I hear that Li Wenyan was injured. As to how serious the injury is, I don\'t know.
- The size and style of this coat are both suitable. As for the color, I think it\'s a bit light.
- I have no interest in medical school, I want to be a policeman. As for my parents, they obviously oppose the idea strongly. But I can\'t help that, I want to be true to myself.
- I am only fastidious about nutritious food. As to whether the taste is good or not, that\'s irrelevant.
- She likes people with good personalities. As for looks, she thinks that\'s unimportant.

**Usage:**

- There are no abrasions with micro-cosmetic surgery, so it can be done anytime. You don\'t need to take into consideration when you have time off.
- This is a small matter. No need to take the boss\'s views into consideration.

### II. 看 — Presenting the Determining Factor

**Function:**

- Whether or not you can pass the exam for the public service, in addition to studying hard, it also depends on luck.
- How long it takes for an injury to heal depends on where the injury is.
- Whether a product is competitive depends on the price. If the price is too high, how is anyone going to want to buy it?
- Whether online sales business will do well depends on the trustworthiness of the seller.
- It\'s hard to say whether tuition in Taiwan is high. It depends on whether the school is private or public.

**Usage:**

- All the sentences above have a "whether... or not" segment, but this is not a redundancy. Sentences below do not.
- 微整型沒有傷口，什麼時候都可以做，不必看什麼時候放假。
- 這是一件小事，不必看老闆的意見吧。

### III. 一向 — Habitual/Always

**Function:**

- He has always liked helping others.
- He has always been confident in his abilities. He won\'t give up.

**Usage:**

1. (From yesterday to today,) He hasn\'t believed what I said. / He has never believed what I say.
2. She always listens to other people\'s suggestions, you\'d better stop saying that. (一直)
3. He always plans ahead before traveling. (一直)
4. The winter here has always been humid, but this year it changed.
5. He has never liked eating seafood, but yesterday he ordered lemon fish.
6. He will walk with you all the way to school. (一向)

### IV. 拿…來說 — Taking...as an Example

**Function:**

- Our company\'s products are highly competitive. Take prices as an example; our products are the cheapest on the market.
- I hate taking part in competitions. Take the last speech contest for example; I was so nervous that I completely forgot everything I was going to say.
- Speeding is often the reason for car accidents. Take Wang Damling\'s accident as an example, his speed was almost twice the stipulated speed.
- This company\'s terms of employment are really good. Take paid vacation for example, you only need to work here two years to set ten days of vacation.
- Lily is very fussy about clothes. Take coats for example, she has ten different styles of coats that are all black.

**Usage:**

- He has always looked after his students. No matter what problems they face, he does everything he can to help.

### V. Passive Sentences with 受

**Function:**

- In Taiwan, teaching in university is a respected line of work.
- Mayday\'s songs are loved by the college crowd. Their concert tickets sell out quickly.
- He is very capable and the boss likes him. He is valued more and more in the company.

(3) This is a fun game and is very popular with high school students. (4) He didn\'t do a good job and was reamed out by the boss. (5) I parked my car on the side of the road last weekend, and it got smashed into. What rotten luck! (6) He walked quietly into the office. He didn\'t want to be noticed.

**Function:**

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### 寒涼平溫熱 Cold, Balanced, and Hot Food

The study of nutrition uses concepts like proteins, calories, carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals when assessing the nutritional value of foods. Chinese medicine, however, attaches importance to food "properties." It divides them into five types — cold (寒 han), cool (涼 liang), balanced (平 ping), warm (溫 wen), and hot (熱 re) — but most generally three types: cold (寒涼), balanced (平性), and hot (溫熱).

Being hot in terms of temperature doesn\'t mean that a food is necessarily hot according to the Chinese medicine definition of the word, nor does being cold in terms of temperature mean that a food is cold to a practitioner of Chinese medicine. Whether a food is hot or cold depends on the body\'s response to it when it is consumed. For example, if a person feels warm after eating mutton pot in the dead of winter, and a person can sweat after eating spicy hot food. From the way the body feels after eating lamb, hot peppers, and garlic, we can know that these foods are hot. If you eat a slice of watermelon or a bowl of mung bean soup in the summer, you immediately feel cool and refreshed. As a result, the ancients categorized watermelon and mung bean soup as cold foods.

People, too, can be divided into hot and cold, depending on their health. People who have cold hands and feet, who like to drink hot liquids, who have pale complexions, and who lack energy often have chronic illnesses. They are considered to have cold constitutions and should eat plenty of warm and hot foods. These foods have such functions as making the spleen healthier, improving appetite, and strengthening the kidneys. In contrast, individuals who radiate heat, don\'t like hot weather, are often thirsty, like to drink cold beverages, have a red complexion, and are constantly agitated frequently experience acute sicknesses or have virus or germ infections. They have hot constitutions and should consume plenty of cool and cold foods. Cool and cold foods have such functions as clearing internal heat, relieving inflammation and fever, as well as detoxifying.

When deciding what to eat, you not only have to take into account your constitution type, you also have to choose foods based on the season. In the summer, you should eat colder foods, like bitter melon, watermelon, and bananas to reduce internal heat. Eating too many warm and hot foods, like lamb, walnuts, and pine nuts, will make you thirsty and give you a sore throat. In the winter, you should eat plenty of hot foods in hot pots and steews. Cold foods, like watermelon, not only can make you feel cool and refreshed; if eaten in excess, they can cause diarrhea. Are you eating right?
