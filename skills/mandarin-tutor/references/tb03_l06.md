# 第六課 到鄉下住一晚

*Dì liù kè — Dào xiāngxià zhù yì wǎn!*

Spending the Night in the Countryside

> **Note on pinyin:** Book 3 prints no romanization; pinyin below is editorial. Vocabulary readings are from the lesson's official vocabulary deck. Zhuyin beside the printed text was illegible in the scan.

## 學習目標 Learning Objectives

**Topic: 台灣農業 Taiwan's Agricultural Industry**

- Learning to discuss the cultivation of agricultural crops.
- Learning to explain your reasons for choosing something.
- Learning to talk about changes in society, including values and lifestyle.
- Learning to discuss food safety issues with friends.

## 對話 Dialogue

*(06-01)*

**（李文彥帶朋友安德思、羅珊蒂去鄉下表哥家）**

*Lǐ Wényàn dài péngyou Āndésī, Luó Shāndì qù xiāngxià biǎogē jiā.*

(Li Wenyan takes his friends An Desi and Luo Shandi to his cousin's house in the country.)

**表哥：** 歡迎、歡迎。大家隨便坐。這是剛摘下來的芒果，大家嚐嚐。

*Biǎogē: Huānyíng, huānyíng. Dàjiā suíbiàn zuò. Zhè shì gāng zhāi xiàlái de mángguǒ, dàjiā chángchang.*

**李文彥：** 很好吃吧？我表哥除了芒果以外，還種了很多有機蔬菜。他是現代農夫。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Hěn hǎochī ba? Wǒ biǎogē chúle mángguǒ yǐwài, hái zhòng le hěn duō yǒujī shūcài. Tā shì xiàndài nóngfū.*

**安德思：** 為什麼說他是現代農夫？

*Āndésī: Wèishénme shuō tā shì xiàndài nóngfū?*

**李文彥：** 因為他跟傳統農夫很不一樣。為了減少對環境的污染，他不用農藥，而且只用天然堆肥，大家可以放心地吃。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Yīnwèi tā gēn chuántǒng nóngfū hěn bù yíyàng. Wèile jiǎnshǎo duì huánjìng de wūrǎn, tā bú yòng nóngyào, érqiě zhǐ yòng tiānrán duīféi, dàjiā kěyǐ fàngxīn de chī.*

**安德思：** 當農夫不是很辛苦嗎？你為什麼要當農夫？

*Āndésī: Dāng nóngfū bú shì hěn xīnkǔ ma? Nǐ wèishénme yào dāng nóngfū?*

**表哥：** 我本來在大學教書，因為看到父母年紀大了，做不動了，才決定回來幫忙的。

*Biǎogē: Wǒ běnlái zài dàxué jiāoshū, yīnwèi kàndào fùmǔ niánjì dà le, zuò bú dòng le, cái juédìng huílái bāngmáng de.*

**羅珊蒂：** 你家人不反對你回來嗎？

*Luó Shāndì: Nǐ jiārén bù fǎnduì nǐ huílái ma?*

**表哥：** 當然反對。農夫在台灣的社會地位不高，工作辛苦不說，收入也不穩定，父母都希望孩子有更好的發展。

*Biǎogē: Dāngrán fǎnduì. Nóngfū zài Táiwān de shèhuì dìwèi bù gāo, gōngzuò xīnkǔ bù shuō, shōurù yě bù wěndìng, fùmǔ dōu xīwàng háizi yǒu gèng hǎo de fāzhǎn.*

**羅珊蒂：** 怎麼會呢？超市的有機蔬菜都貴得不得了。

*Luó Shāndì: Zěnme huì ne? Chāoshì de yǒujī shūcài dōu guì de bùdéliǎo.*

**表哥：** 賣給超市，農夫的利潤很低，可是自己賣的話，問題更多。今天不談這個。天氣這麼好，別一直待在屋子裡，我們出去走走吧。

*Biǎogē: Mài gěi chāoshì, nóngfū de lìrùn hěn dī, kěshì zìjǐ mài de huà, wèntí gèng duō. Jīntiān bù tán zhège. Tiānqì zhème hǎo, bié yìzhí dāi zài wūzi lǐ, wǒmen chūqù zǒuzou ba.*

**（到了屋外）**

*(Dào le wūwài.)*

**安德思：** 這些番茄真漂亮！

*Āndésī: Zhèxiē fānqié zhēn piàoliang!*

**表哥：** 這不是基因改造的，是新品種。今年氣候特別好，再加上沒有颱風，所以青菜水果都長得很好。

*Biǎogē: Zhè bú shì jīyīn gǎizào de, shì xīn pǐnzhǒng. Jīnnián qìhòu tèbié hǎo, zài jiāshàng méiyǒu táifēng, suǒyǐ qīngcài shuǐguǒ dōu zhǎng de hěn hǎo.*

**安德思：** 這些番茄熟了嗎？

*Āndésī: Zhèxiē fānqié shóu le ma?*

**表哥：** 熟了，熟了，摘下來就可以吃了。這些番茄都是有機的，不必洗。

*Biǎogē: Shóu le, shóu le, zhāi xiàlái jiù kěyǐ chī le. Zhèxiē fānqié dōu shì yǒujī de, bú bì xǐ.*

**安德思：** 這麼大的一片田，你一個人照顧，忙得過來嗎？

*Āndésī: Zhème dà de yí piàn tián, nǐ yí ge rén zhàogù, máng de guòlái ma?*

**表哥：** 平常還好，可是收成的時候，很難找到人幫忙。

*Biǎogē: Píngcháng hái hǎo, kěshì shōuchéng de shíhou, hěn nán zhǎodào rén bāngmáng.*

**李文彥：** 這幾年，回鄉下種田的年輕人不是很多嗎？

*Lǐ Wényàn: Zhè jǐ nián, huí xiāngxià zhòngtián de niánqīng rén bú shì hěn duō ma?*

**表哥：** 忙的時候，大家都一樣，連自己的事都做不完，怎麼有時間幫別人的忙？

*Biǎogē: Máng de shíhou, dàjiā dōu yíyàng, lián zìjǐ de shì dōu zuò bù wán, zěnme yǒu shíjiān bāng biérén de máng?*

**羅珊蒂：** 你怎麼不找個太太來幫你？

*Luó Shāndì: Nǐ zěnme bù zhǎo ge tàitai lái bāng nǐ?*

**李文彥：** 妳這問題問得真好。我表哥什麼都有，就是少了一個太太。

*Lǐ Wényàn: Nǐ zhè wèntí wèn de zhēn hǎo. Wǒ biǎogē shénme dōu yǒu, jiùshì shǎo le yí ge tàitai.*

**表哥：** 別開玩笑了。農夫看天吃飯，收入這麼不穩定，怎麼敢結婚？

*Biǎogē: Bié kāi wánxiào le. Nóngfū kàn tiān chīfàn, shōurù zhème bù wěndìng, zěnme gǎn jiéhūn?*

**羅珊蒂：** 表哥，你條件這麼好，我給你介紹一個，怎麼樣？

*Luó Shāndì: Biǎogē, nǐ tiáojiàn zhème hǎo, wǒ gěi nǐ jièshào yí ge, zěnmeyàng?*

**表哥：** 以後再說吧！天黑了，我們快回去吃飯。

*Biǎogē: Yǐhòu zài shuō ba! Tiān hēi le, wǒmen kuài huíqù chīfàn.*

### Text in Simplified Characters

#### 现代小农

星期天一大早，何雅婷就陪着妈妈到农夫市集去买菜。最近食品安全出了好几次问题，何雅婷的妈妈为了家人的健康，开始注意食材的产地，也尽量到有机商店和农夫市集买菜。虽然这些地方卖的东西种类没有市场那么多，价钱也比较高，可是妈妈常跟农夫聊天，了解小农用友善的方式对待土地和环境，很信任他们，所以宁可每个星期跑一趟，多花一点钱，也要支持小农。

像何雅婷妈妈这样关心小农的人越来越多。有人写文章介绍小农种的蔬菜水果，有人到处帮小农推销，其中最有名的是一位面包师傅。他从小在农村长大，了解农夫的辛苦。为了帮助小农，也为了帮自己的产品找更好的食材，他常常拜访小农，用他们出产的食材做出受欢迎的面包。

由于报纸、网路的介绍，许多住在城市里的人开始羡慕小农的生活。他们利用放假的时候，带著孩子到农村去，一方面可以让孩子在田里跑跑跳跳，接近自然，一方面自己也可以放松心情。到乡下住一晚变成了现在热门的休闲活动。

随著小农越来越受重视，到农村来观光的人也慢慢地多起来了，连便利商店都来了。以前当地人习惯自己做饭，或是到传统的小商店和小吃店消费，现在便利商店更方便，也更吸引他们。传统的小店生意受到了影响，原来安静的农村有了很大的变化。现在农人担心的是传统的生活方式会不会消失。

### English

(In the country.)

Cousin: Welcome, welcome. Sit wherever you like. These are freshly picked mangoes. Have a taste.

Li Wenyan: Really tasty, right? In addition to mangoes, my cousin has planted a lot of organic vegetables. He's a modern farmer.

An Desi: Why do you say he's a modern farmer?

Li Wenyan: Because he's very different from traditional farmers. In order to reduce environmental pollution, he doesn't use pesticides and only uses natural compost. People can eat with their minds at ease.

An Desi: Isn't being a farmer tough? Why did you want to become a farmer?

Cousin: I used to teach at a university. It was only because I saw that my parents were old and couldn't work that I decided to come back and help out.

Luo Shandi: Your family didn't oppose you returning?

Cousin: Of course they were against it. The social status of farmers in Taiwan isn't high. The hard work aside, you don't have a stable income. Parents all hope that their children have better prospects.

Luo Shandi: How is that possible? Organic vegetables in the supermarket are all incredibly expensive.

Cousin: Farmers' profits are low when they sell to supermarkets, but if you sell them yourself, you have even more problems. Let's not talk about this today. The weather is so good; we shouldn't stay inside all day. Let's go out and walk around.

An Desi: These tomatoes are really pretty!

Cousin: These aren't genetically modified; they're a new variety. The climate has been especially good this year, plus there haven't been any typhoons, so the vegetables and fruit have grown really well.

An Desi: Are these tomatoes ripe?

Cousin: Yes, they're ripe. You can eat them right off the plant. These tomatoes are organic; there's no need to wash them.

An Desi: Such a large plot of land. Can you manage it all alone?

Cousin: Generally, it's not a problem, but during harvest time it's hard to find people to help.

Li Wenyan: Over the past few years, haven't a lot of young people been returning to the countryside to till the land?

Cousin: When it's busy, everybody's the same. You can't even finish your own work. How can you have time to help others?

Luo Shandi: Why don't you find a wife to help you?

Li Wenyan: That's a really good question. My cousin has everything, except a wife.

Cousin: Don't joke around. A farmer's livelihood depends on the whims of nature, and the income is so unpredictable. How can I dare get married?

Luo Shandi: Cousin, you've got so much going for you. I'll introduce someone to you. What do you think?

Cousin: Maybe later! It's getting dark. Let's hurry back and eat.

### English

#### Small Farmers in Modern Times

Early Sunday morning, He Yating accompanied her mom to the farmers' market to buy produce. Lately, there have been several problems with food safety. For the sake of her family's health, He Yating's mom has begun paying attention to where the food comes from. She does her best to shop at organic stores and farmers' markets. These places don't have as many kinds of products as regular markets, and the prices are higher, but her mom often chats with the farmers and learns about the environmentally friendly ways small farmers treat the land and environment. She trusts them, so she'd rather make the trip once a week and spend a little more money in order to support small farmers.

More and more people, like He Yating's mom, care about small farmers. Some people write articles introducing the fruits and vegetables they grow; others go around helping small farmers market their products. The most famous of these is a master baker. He grew up in a farming village and understands how hard a farmer's life is. To help small farmers and find better ingredients for his own products, he often visits them and uses their produce to make popular breads.

Because of coverage in newspapers and on the internet, many people who live in cities have begun to envy the lives of small farmers. On their days off, they take their children to farming villages. On the one hand, the children can run around in the fields and get close to nature; on the other, the adults can relax. Spending a night in the countryside has become a popular leisure activity.

As small farmers receive more and more attention, increasing numbers of people are coming to farming villages to sightsee—even convenience stores have arrived. In the past, local people were accustomed to cooking for themselves or shopping at traditional little stores and eateries. Now convenience stores are more convenient and more attractive to them. The businesses of traditional small stores have been affected, and once-quiet farming villages have changed a great deal. Farmers now worry that traditional ways of life may disappear.

## 文法 Grammar

### I. 因為…才… only because…

*(06-05)*

**Function:** This pattern stresses that an event happened only because of a particular reason. It is a typical cause-and-effect construction.

**Structure:** 因為 + reason，subject + 才 + result。

- 因為賣保險，收入不穩定，她才想換工作的。
- 因為這個沙拉是用表哥種的有機蔬菜做的，我才吃的。
- 美美條件很好，因為工作一直很忙，才到現在還沒有結婚。
- 因為工作壓力太大，健康出了問題，他才決定回鄉下種田的。
- 因為他不但熱心，而且成績好，才拿到獎學金的。

**Usage:** 才 is used here for a past event. 因為…所以… also expresses cause and effect and is not restricted to past events.

**Examples:**

- A：你不是最喜歡看煙火的嗎？怎麼沒去？
- B：因為我不舒服，才沒去看煙火的。
- A：你明天為什麼不去看煙火了？
- B：因為要去女朋友家，所以不能去看煙火了。

### II. 不說…也… not to mention…; on top of that…

*(06-06)*

**Function:** This pattern complains about two undesirable things; the second is usually more serious.

**Structure:** S1 + 不說，S2 + 也 + negative comment。

- 那個地方吵不說，環境也很複雜。
- 他現在的工作是推銷產品。薪水低不說，也很辛苦。
- 那家店賣的商品種類少不說，價錢也很貴，難怪客人不多。
- 小林最近常遲到不說，功課也不寫。他怎麼了？
- 週年慶的時候，擠死了不說，也常常因為便宜買了一些沒有用的東西回家。

**Usage:** Like 不但…還…, this adds one fact to another. 不但…還… can introduce positive or negative points; 不說…也… is used for complaints about negative situations.

### I. 因為…才…

- She only wants to change jobs because she sells insurance and her income isn't stable.
- I'm only eating this salad because it's made from organic vegetables raised by my cousin.

- Xiao Lin has been late a lot recently; not to mention, he hasn't been doing his homework. What's up with him?

- The style of that garment was relatively old and the color too light. In addition, it was uncomfortable, so even though it was half off, I didn't buy it.

### IV. 什麼都…就是…

- This shop has 20% off everything except the things I want to buy today.

### V. 寧可…也要…

### VI. 像…的 + noun

- He likes all kinds of light snacks, like steamed dumplings, fried chicken fillet, and danzai noodles.

### VII. 一方面…一方面…

- She went to the Mayday concert, on the one hand, to kick back and relax, and on the other, to figure out why the band is so popular.
- She's working part-time during the summer, on the one hand, to make a little money, and on the other, to get a little experience in society.

- Going back to your hometown during holidays lets you, on the one hand, get together with your family, and on the other, see old schoolmates.

### VIII. 隨著…，…也…

- As China's economy improves, the country's international position becomes increasingly important.
- As her Chinese improves, she is taking part in more and more activities.
- As people come to better understand pesticides, an increasing number of people are buying organic products.
- As the date of the graduate-school entrance exam draws nearer, he grows increasingly stressed.
- As she gets older, she thinks more and more about her child working in the United States.

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### 胡、番、洋 — Words for Things from Abroad

*[Printed in English in the textbook; Chinese terms are shown inline.]*

A student asked me today, “Why doesn't the 番 (*fān*) in 番茄 (*fānqié*, tomato) have a grass radical above it? Aren't tomatoes plants?” His question shows how advanced his Chinese is. The problem is that 番 has more to do with history, so it's understandable why he asked. Chinese terms with 胡 (*hú*), 番 (*fān*), and 洋 (*yáng*) describe things produced or invented outside of China; they were introduced from abroad.

Of these three terms, 胡 has the longest history. During the Qin and Han Dynasties (221 BC–220 AD), the 胡人 (*húrén*) were China's strongest enemy. The term referred to either the Beidi or the Huns, both ethnic minorities in the north. Later, Zhang Qian of the Han Dynasty was sent as an envoy to the Western Region, opening a road to the West known as the Silk Road and bringing frequent exchange between East and West. According to historical documents, Eastern Han Emperor Ling (東漢靈帝 *Dōng Hàn Língdì*) was fond of foreign clothes (胡服 *húfú*), foreign beds (胡床 *húchuáng*), foreign food (胡飯 *húfàn*), and the like. China saw the “foreignization” of society as Chinese people wore foreign clothes and integrated foreign foods into their diet. The foreign “beds” (胡床) were actually seats invented by foreigners, which brought the Chinese custom of sitting on the floor to an end. Other things from the Western Region include black pepper (胡椒 *hújiāo*), walnuts (胡桃 *hútáo*), sesame (胡麻 *húmá*), cucumbers (胡瓜 *húguā*), carrots (胡蘿蔔 *húluóbo*), and the huqin (胡琴 *húqín*), a two-stringed bowed instrument.

When ethnic groups from China's north invaded the Central Plains, many people fled southward. People arriving from the north no longer used 胡 for imported items; instead, they used 番. Tomatoes (番茄), sweet potatoes (番薯 *fānshǔ*), corn (番麥 *fānmài*), hot peppers (番椒 *fānjiāo*), and guava (番石榴 *fānshíliú*), which arrived in China indirectly from the New World via Europe during the Age of Discovery, were all prefixed with 番. This is why 番 in 番茄 is not written with a grass radical.

洋 was the latest character used in naming imported items. As the name suggests, 洋 means “ocean”; items prefixed with it crossed the ocean to reach China. Examples include onions (洋蔥 *yángcōng*), western mushrooms (洋菇 *yánggū*), potatoes (洋芋 *yángyù*), and western orchids (洋蘭 *yánglán*). Of the three terms, 洋 is the least derogatory. One might say of a scholar, 他喝過洋墨水，見識比較廣 (*Tā hē guò yáng mòshuǐ, jiànshì bǐjiào guǎng*; literally, “He has drunk western ink”—he studied abroad and has broad knowledge and experience). Of an adorable child, one might say, 他的孩子好可愛，真像洋娃娃 (*Tā de háizi hǎo kě'ài, zhēn xiàng yángwáwa*; “His child is lovely, like a doll”). 胡 in phrases never means anything good. Examples include 胡說 (*húshuō*, nonsense), 胡鬧 (*húnào*, to run wild), and 胡作非為 (*húzuò-fēiwéi*, to act wildly in defiance of rules or law). The same is true of 番: in the past, Indigenous peoples were divided into “savage tribes” (生番 *shēngfān*) and “assimilated border tribes” (熟番 *shúfān*). During the colonial period, Westerners were called “Red-haired Foreigners” (紅毛番 *hóngmáofān*), and their books were called 番仔書 (*fānzǎishū*, foreign books).

|---|---|---|
| Carrot, pepper, cucumber, and walnut include 胡, indicating origin in the Western Region (today's western China). | Corn, tomato, hot pepper, sweet potato, and guava include 番, indicating origin among ethnic groups in China's north. | Onion, potato, and mushroom include 洋, indicating that they were brought across the ocean to China. |
