## 對話 Dialogue 06-01

> （高來恩和李文麗討論地震經驗）

**高來恩：** 昨天晚上七點左右有地震，妳知道嗎？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Zuótiān wǎnshang qī diǎn zuǒyǒu dìzhèn, nǐ zhīdao ma?*

**李文麗：** 那時候我正在車上，完全沒感覺到。我是後來看新聞才知道的。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Nà shíhou wǒ zhèngzài chē shàng, wánquán méi gǎnjuédào. Wǒ shì hòulái kàn xīnwén cái zhīdao de.*

**高來恩：** 我住在二十樓，搖得好厲害。地震怎麼這麼可怕啊！

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Wǒ zhù zài èrshí lóu, yáo de hǎo lìhai. Dìzhèn zěnme zhème kěpà a!*

**李文麗：** 不要怕，這裡經常有地震，我們都已經習慣了，昨天的地震不算大。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Búyào pà, zhèlǐ jīngcháng yǒu dìzhèn, wǒmen dōu yǐjīng xíguàn le, zuótiān de dìzhèn búsuàn dà.*

**高來恩：** 聽說有一次台灣發生大地震，很多房子倒了，橋斷了。多恐怖啊！

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Tīngshuō yǒu yícì Táiwān fāshēng dàdìzhèn, hěn duō fángzi dǎole, qiáo duànle. Duō kǒngbù a!*

**李文麗：** 那一次的大地震，有很多人受傷了，還有很多人被壓死了。台灣中部災情慘重，台北也有一棟大樓倒了。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Nà yícì de dàdìzhèn, yǒu hěn duō rén shòushāng le, hái yǒu hěn duō rén bèi yāsǐle. Táiwān zhōngbù zāiqíng cǎnzhòng, Táiběi yǒu yī dòng dàlóu dǎole.*

**高來恩：** 在我的國家從來沒有發生過地震，對我來說，這是一個很特別的經驗。

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Zài wǒ de guójiā cónglái méiyǒu fāshēngguo dìzhèn, duì wǒ lái shuō, zhè shì yíge hěn tèbié de jīngyàn.*

**李文麗：** 西歐、北歐、中歐都沒有地震，澳洲幾乎沒有地震，南美洲東半部也很少。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Xī Ōu, Běi Ōu, Zhōng Ōu dōu méiyǒu dìzhèn, Àozhōu jīhū méiyǒu dìzhèn, Nán Měizhōu dōng bàn bù yě hěn shǎo.*

**高來恩：** 哪些地區常發生地震呢？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Nǎxiē dìqū cháng fāshēng dìzhèn ne?*

**李文麗：** 根據地球科學研究，世界上發生地震的位置大部分都在地震帶上。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Gēnjù dìqiú kēxué yánjiū, shìjiè shàng fāshēng dìzhèn de wèizhì dàbùfèn dōu zài dìzhèn dài shàng.*

**高來恩：** 台灣的地震那麼多，台灣一定是在地震帶上。

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Táiwān de dìzhèn nàme duō, Táiwān yídìng shì zài dìzhèn dài shàng.*

**李文麗：** 沒錯，像台灣、日本、菲律賓這些國家都在地震帶上，所以發生強烈地震的頻率相當高。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Méicuò, xiàng Táiwān, Rìběn, Fēilǜbīn zhèxiē guójiā dōu zài dìzhèn dài shàng, suǒyǐ fāshēng qiángliè dìzhèn de pínlǜ xiāngdāng gāo.*

**高來恩：** 現在颱風都可以預測了，地震應該也可以預測吧？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Xiànzài táifēng dōu kěyǐ yùcè le, dìzhèn yīnggāi yě kěyǐ yùcè ba?*

**李文麗：** 有人说地震快來的時候，動物會出現一些徵兆，在人類還沒有感覺震動前，鳥類已經對地震有感覺了，像雞就可以預先知道。其他像老鼠、貓、狗、馬……等等，也會預先知道，可是因為都沒有經過證實，所以到現在地震還是沒辦法預測。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Yǒurén shuō dìzhèn kuài lái de shíhou, dòngwù huì chūxiàn yīxiē zhēngzhào, zài rénlèi hái méiyǒu gǎnjué zhèndòng qián, niǎolèi yǐjīng duì dìzhèn yǒu gǎnjué le, xiàng jī jiù kěyǐ yùxiān zhīdao. Qítā xiàng lǎoshǔ, māo, gǒu, mǎ……děngděng, yě huì yùxiān zhīdao, kěshì yīnwèi dōu méiyǒu jīngguò zhèngshí, suǒyǐ dào xiànzài dìzhèn hái shì méi bànfǎ yùcè.*

**高來恩：** 既然地震是沒辦法預測的，我們應該怎麼辦？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Jìrán dìzhèn shì méi bànfǎ yùcè de, wǒmen yīnggāi zěnme bàn?*

**李文麗：** 我們平常要隨時做好準備，萬一發生地震就知道怎麼應變了。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Wǒmen píngcháng yào suíshí zuòhǎo zhǔnbèi, wànyī fāshēng dìzhèn jiù zhīdao zěnme yìngbiàn le.*

**高來恩：** 那麼我們要怎麼做準備呢？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Nàme wǒmen yào zěnme zuò zhǔnbèi ne?*

**李文麗：** 家中要準備一個救生包，就放在隨手拿得到的地方。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Jiāzhōng yào zhǔnbèi yíge jiùshēng bāo, jiù fàng zài suíshǒu ná dedào de dìfang.*

**高來恩：** 可是如果大地震沒發生，那我不是白準備了嗎？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Kěshì rúguǒ dà dìzhèn méi fāshēng, nà wǒ búshì bái zhǔnbèi le ma?*

**李文麗：** 不要嫌麻煩，有備無患，事前準備總是比較好啊！

*Lǐ Wénlì: Búyào xián máfán, yǒubèi wúhuàn, shìqiān zhǔnbèi zǒngshì bǐjiào hǎo a!*

### English

**Gao Lai'en:** Yesterday evening around seven there was an earthquake. Did you know?

**Li Wenli:** At that time I was in a car. I couldn't feel anything at all. I only found out from the news later.

**Gao Lai'en:** I live on the 20th floor. It shook terribly. How scary the earthquake was!

**Li Wenli:** Don't be afraid. We get earthquakes here all the time — we're already used to them. Yesterday's wasn't that big.

**Gao Lai'en:** I heard a big earthquake hit Taiwan once. Many buildings collapsed, bridges broke. How terrifying!

**Li Wenli:** That quake hurt a lot of people and crushed many to death. Central Taiwan took the worst hit; even a building in Taipei fell.

**Gao Lai'en:** In my country there's never been an earthquake. For me it's a very unusual experience.

**Li Wenli:** Western, Northern, and Central Europe have none; Australia hardly any; South America's eastern half rarely sees one either.

**Gao Lai'en:** Which regions get earthquakes often?

**Li Wenli:** According to earth science research, most quakes happen along seismic zones.

**Gao Lai'en:** Taiwan gets so many quakes — it must be on a seismic zone.

**Li Wenli:** Right. Taiwan, Japan, the Philippines — all on seismic zones, so the frequency of strong quakes is high.

**Gao Lai'en:** Typhoons can be predicted now, so earthquakes should be predictable too, right?

**Li Wenli:** They say animals sense quakes before humans do — birds, for instance, know in advance. Mice, cats, dogs, horses — all supposedly. But none of it's been verified, so prediction's still impossible.

**Gao Lai'en:** If we can't predict them, what should we do?

**Li Wenli:** Keep a survival kit ready at all times. If a quake hits, you'll know how to respond.

**Gao Lai'en:** So how do we prepare?

**Li Wenli:** Keep a survival kit at home, somewhere within easy reach.

**Gao Lai'en:** But if no big quake ever comes, I've wasted my time preparing, right?

**Li Wenli:** Don't be lazy — better safe than sorry. Preparation's always worth it!

## 短文 Reading 06-03

### 地震時如何逃生

（李文麗和高來恩去聽演講）

大家好！今天我很高興到這裡來跟大家談一談地震發生時如何逃生及應變。雖然現在科技已經很發達了，但是地震還是沒有辦法避免的天災，我們應該隨時做好準備，才可以讓災害減少到最低的程度。

首先我們在家裡要準備一個救生包，其次要練習如何在災難發生時逃生。救生包裡面應該放一些緊急時的必需品，譬如手電筒、水、餅乾等等。平時要和家人練習怎麼逃生，知道各種狀況發生時應該怎麼辦，這樣萬一發生地震才不會慌張。要是晚上停電，我們得在黑暗中找到逃生的方向，也應該先和家人約好逃生後集合的地點，這是平常可以練習的。

另外要檢查房子有沒有裂縫？家裡的擺飾會不會在地震時掉下來？當地震發生時，逃生前應該先關瓦斯開關，才不會發生火災；還要把門打開，免得因為門框歪了，打不開門。地震的時候，如果人在房子裡，要趕快找東西保護頭部。逃生時最重要的是冷靜，如果一慌張，就容易錯過逃生的機會。

如果地震非常厲害，大到連站都站不穩，這時候不要急著往外跑，因為這時可能會有櫃子倒下來，甚至牆壁也會倒塌。急著往外面跑，或是搭電梯下樓，不但不安全，反而更危險。一棟房子最堅固的地方就是柱子，所以柱子的旁邊是比較安全的。也有專家建議，可以躲在比較堅固的桌子旁邊，那麼當櫃子或牆壁倒下來的時候會先碰到桌子，形成一個保護自己的三角形空間。

如果地震發生後造成很嚴重的災害，我們要讓自己盡量保持平靜，讓最需要幫助的人先得到照顧，要聽救災人員的指示，這樣災後的救援行動和重建工作才會更順利。

地震时如何逃生

（李文丽和高来恩去听演讲）

大家好！今天我很高兴到这里来跟大家谈一谈地震发生时如何逃生及应变。虽然现在科技已经很发达了，但是地震还是没有办法避免的天灾，我们应该随时做好准备，才可以让灾害减少到最低的程度。

首先我们在家里要准备一个救生包，其次要练习如何在灾难发生时逃生。救生包里面应该放一些紧急时的必需品，譬如手电筒、水、饼干等等。平时要和家人练习怎么逃生，知道各种状况发生时应该怎么办，这样万一发生地震才不会慌张。要是晚上停电，我们得在黑暗中找到逃生的方向，也应该先和家人约好逃生后集合的地点，这是平常可以练习的。

如果地震发生后造成很严重的灾害，我们要让自己尽量保持平静，让最需要帮助的人先得到照顾，要听救灾人员的指示，这样灾后的救援行动和重建工作才会更顺利。

### English

**How to Evacuate During an Earthquake**

(Li Wenli and Gao Lai'en attending a lecture)

Good afternoon! I'm very happy to be here today to talk about how to evacuate and respond when an earthquake strikes. Although technology is now very advanced, earthquakes remain a natural disaster we cannot avoid. We should always be prepared so that we can minimize damage when one occurs.

First, keep a survival kit at home. Second, practice how to evacuate when a disaster strikes. The kit should contain essentials for emergencies — a flashlight, water, crackers, and so on. Practice with your family how to evacuate and what to do in various situations, so that if an earthquake does come, you won't panic. If the power goes out at night, find your way in the dark, and agree on a meeting place with your family beforehand — something you can practice anytime.

Also check whether your house has cracks? Will your decorations fall during an earthquake? When an earthquake strikes, turn off the gas switch before evacuating to prevent fires; also open the door in case the frame warps and you can't open it. When the quake hits, if you're indoors, quickly protect your head. The most important thing during evacuation is to stay calm — if you panic, you'll miss your chance.

If the earthquake is so strong you can't even stand, don't rush outside — cabinets may topple, even walls may collapse. Running outside or taking the elevator is dangerous; in fact, it's even riskier. The sturdiest part of a building is the columns, so standing beside a column is relatively safe. Experts also suggest hiding under a sturdy table — when cabinets or walls fall, they'll hit the table first, forming a protective triangular space.

If a quake causes serious damage, stay as calm as possible. Help those who need it most first, follow rescue personnel's instructions, and the rescue and rebuilding will go more smoothly.

### I. 怎麼這麼 why so...? — 06-05

**Function:** These two adverbs, 怎麼 and 這麼, are combined to convey the speaker's amazement. Only state verbs follow them.

- A: 聽說小王每天都去夜市吃雞排。
  B: 他怎麼這麼愛吃雞排？！
- A: 這件羊毛外套一件賣三萬塊錢。
  B: 怎麼這麼貴？！
- A: 這位女明星得了今年的最佳女主角獎。
  B: 她還這麼年輕，演技怎麼這麼好！
- A: 在荷蘭就算下雪，孩子們也會到戶外活動。
  B: 怎麼這麼不怕冷？！
- A: 下訂單以後24小時就能收到訂購的東西。
  B: 怎麼這麼快？！

### II. Intensifying Adverb 多 how — 06-06

**Function:** 多 expresses a high level of intensity, an exaggeration, or charged emotion. It is frequently used at the end of a sentence, often followed by the particle 呀 or 啊.

- 這裡的風景多美啊！真是百聞不如一見。
- 你看，老闆給我的薪水這麼少，多小氣呀！

- 這棟建築多堅固啊！地震以後只有它沒倒。

### III. The Various Functions of 多 — 06-07

**Function:**

1. **Vs, abundant (productive use).** 多 in this function can be used either as a main verb (predicator) or as a modifier. In both functions, it must be preceded by 彳艮, e.g.,
   - (1) 經濟情況好的時候，就業機會很多。(as main verb)
   - (2) 在這個時代，選擇網路購物的人很多。(as main verb)
   - (3) 和平東路上有很多便利商店。(as modifier)
   - (4) 捷運站或公園裡都有很多裝置藝術。(as modifier)

2. **Vs, various, numerous (unproductive).** These are isolated facts and must be learned as such.
   - (1) 多 followed by measures, e.g., 多種，多年,多樣.
     - a. 多位教授都認為應該改變目前的教育制度。
     - b. 網路商店裡的衣服有多種款式。
   - (2) 多 in 4-character frames (四字格), e.g., 多子多孫, 多災多難, 多才多藝.

3. **Adv. (productive)**
   - (1) primarily serving as an adverbial occurring before a VP, e.g.,
     - a. 我已經訂了豬腳麵線和蛋。等一下你多吃一點。(B1,L13)
     - b. 我還得多學一點。(B2,L1)
     - c. 沒想到二十年以後還能見面，以後有時間要多聯絡。
   - (2) 多 can also be used as an adverbial complement, occurring after verbs (productive). In this function, it typically associates with 了, e.g.,
     - a. 漢堡吃多了對健康不好。(B2,L12)
     - b. 謝謝妳，好多了。我吃了一包藥以後，睡得比昨天好。(B1,L15)
     - c. 對小玲來說，網路的虛擬關係比現實生活中的關係簡單多了。(B4,L1)
   - (3) 多 reduplicated, unproductive, e.g., 多多幫忙，多多鼓勵，多多支持.
     - a. 麻煩老師多多照顧這個孩子。
     - b. 您真有學問，我要多多跟您學習。
     - c. 要學好中文得多多練習。

### IV. 白 in vain, for nothing — 06-08

**Function:**

1. This adverb modifies action verbs and carries the meaning "to do the action in vain," "to have no effect at all."
   - 我說了那麼多次，你還是聽不懂，我真是白說了。
   - 媽媽做了好多菜，可是家人沒回來吃，媽媽白做了。
   - 我們寫的報告老師說不用交了，我們白寫了。
   - 他不知道學校放颱風假，沒開門，他白去了。
   - 打折的時候買的一些東西都沒用，我都白買了。

2. When used with action verbs such as 吃的, 喝的, and 住的, in contexts where the subject normally pays to do the action, 白 + action verb means to do the action without paying for it, coupled with bad attitude and behavior.
   - 有些餐廳常碰到白吃的客人，老闆覺得很頭痛。
   - 他已經六個月沒付房租了，房東說不能再讓他白住了。

### VI. The Various Meanings of the Verb Complement 下來 — 06-10

**Function:** When 下來 serves as a complement to a verb, forming V+下來, the meaning can be locational (downward, upward), temporal (to continue, to keep on), or a state (remain, still).

**1. Locational**

- 王太太聽見王先生在樓下叫她，就從樓上走下來了。
- 那本書在櫃子上，請你把那本書從櫃子上拿下來。

**2. Temporal**

- 他從十年前來這家公司，一直做下來，到現在已經當主任了。
- 端午節喝雄黃酒、戴香包是古代的人傳下來的習慣。

**3. State**

- 朋友想了很久，最後決定留下來。
- 畫家把鄉下美麗的風景畫下來。

**Usage:** Typical V1V2V3 can occur in both forms or modes:

1. **Actual form:** V下來，沒V下來
   - 我把老闆說的話都寫下來了。
   - 你把髒衣服脫下來洗一洗。
   - 雖然他看見山上有凶猛的動物，可是他還在山上，他沒跑下來。
2. **Potential form:** V得下來，V不下來
   - 月亮怎麼摘得下來？
   - 車開得太快，停不下來。
   - 那個衣櫥太大，我一個人搬不下來。

### VII. 當…的時候 when, while, at the time of — 06-11

**Function:** The preposition 當 refers to the time of an event, represented by a sentence in this case.

- 當機會來的時候，你千萬別錯過。
- 她身材那麼好，當我第一次看到她的時候，我以為她是模特兒。
- 當別人對你說話時，你要仔細聽。
- 當他知道他得到獎學金時，高興地跳起來。
- 當你到國家戲劇院欣賞表演時，一定要關手機。

**Usage:** 當 is often optional in conversations, e.g.,

1. （當）孩子回家的時候，媽媽已經做了很多好吃的菜。
2. （當）來恩住在香港的時候，認識了很多香港朋友。
3. （當）她寫功課時，發現了不少問題。

### VIII. 反而 on the contrary — 06-12

**Function:** The adverb 反而 introduces an action or situation that is contrary to the expectations of the speaker. 反而 brings in S2.

- 你說了那麼多次，她不但沒聽懂，反而更糊塗了。
- 她本來很好看，化了妝以後反而不好看。
- 看了我寫的文章，老師不但沒罵我，反而說我寫得很好。
- 我運動以後不但不累，反而更有精神。
- 現在很多年輕人喜歡穿黑色的，反而是年紀大的人喜歡穿紅色的。

**Usage:** The 反而 clause is often preceded by a 不但 clause (S1 in the negative).

1. 他常常熬夜念書，成績不但沒有進步，反而退步了。
2. 他去當兵不但沒變瘦，反而胖了。
3. 我幫她打掃房間，她不但不覺得高興，反而生氣了。

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### Taiwan's Funeral Customs

After living in Taiwan long enough, many foreigners come into contact with the local funeral culture, and it never fails to leave a lasting impression. According to custom, when someone passes away, family members of the deceased burn paper money in the mourning hall. They believe that this paper money can be used by the deceased in the afterlife.

In addition to paper money, there are many other burnable implements that are offered to ensure a good afterlife. Families can buy paper items of all kinds from the funeral parlor shop, ranging from smaller items like glasses, watches, and cell phones, to bigger ones like computers, houses, beds, fridges, TVs, stereo systems, cars, and scooters — everything one could possibly need.

Traditionally speaking, funerals in Taiwan also incorporate "professional mourners," which are women hired specifically to weep and wail. It is said that, a long time ago, if an elder family member passed away and their daughter was unable to come home in time for the funeral, she would have to hire a filial woman to wail on her behalf. Even today, this custom of substitute mourners is adhered to in Taiwan, where they are called 孝女白琴 (Xiao Nu Bai Qin), or "Bai Qin the Filial Daughter." The name originates from 孝女白瓊 (Xiao Ni Bai Qiong), a character in the Taiwanese glove puppet play 《雲州大儒俠》(The Scholar Swordsman), but the pronunciation of the last character of her name (Qiong) has evolved into the current form, "Qin."

In traditional funerals, filial sons and daughters refer to the children of the deceased; they are to deliver the eulogy should an elder family member pass away. If the deceased has no children, a professional funeral director acts as a substitute, while some families, even those with surviving children, will still hire a professional mourner to make the funeral seem more somber.

Present-day funeral processes are much simpler than those in the days of old. Friends and family go to the home of the deceased and offer their condolences by giving money in a white envelope, with the first number of the total amount always being odd. The grieving family returns the favor by giving a towel or handkerchief. At the end of a funeral guests are to leave quietly, and they are not supposed to turn around. Likewise, family of the deceased are also not supposed to say "see you again," because death is a grievous thing, and not something anyone wants "again."

Other possible burnt offerings include liquor, Mahjong, model vehicles, etc. Those offering condolences give the mourning family a white envelope containing an odd-numbered amount of money. The family gives them a towel in return.
