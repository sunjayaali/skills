## 對話 Dialogue 09-01

> （在台北東區）

**高來恩：** 哇！台北東區好熱鬧啊！

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Wà! Táiběi dōng qū hǎo rènào a!*

**李文麗：** 以前這裡可不是熱鬧的地方，一百年前這裡還是鄉下，沒什麼人住。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Yǐqián zhèlǐ kě búshì rènào de dìfāng, yībǎinián qián zhèlǐ hái shì xiāngxià, méi shénme rén zhù.*

**高來恩：** 台灣以前最熱鬧的地方在哪裡？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Táiwān yǐqián zuì rènào de dìfāng zài nǎli?*

**李文麗：** 台灣的政治、經濟中心是從南往北逐漸發展的。以前有三個最熱鬧的地方，一是台南，二是鹿港，三是艋舺。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Táiwān de zhèngzhì, jīngjì zhōngxīn shì cóng nán wǎng běi zhújiàn fāzhǎn de. Yǐqián yǒu sān gè zuì rènào de dìfāng, yī shì Táinán, èr shì Lùgǎng, sān shì Měngxiá.*

**高來恩：** 我去過台南，我知道差不多四百年前，荷蘭人曾經把台灣當做亞洲的貿易據點。台南有不少古蹟，赤嵌樓就是荷蘭人統治台灣時，在台南建造的，那是家喻戶曉的觀光景點。鹿港和艋舺在哪裡？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Wǒ guò qù Táinán, wǒ zhīdào chàbuduō sì bǎinián qián, Hélán rén céngjīng bǎ Táiwān dàngzuò Yàzhōu de màoyì jùdiǎn. Táinán bùshǎo gǔjī, Chìqiànlóu jiùshì Hélán rén tǒngzhì Táiwān shí, zài Táinán jiàozào de, nà shì jiāyù hùxiǎo de guānjǐng jīngdiǎn. Lùgǎng hé Měngxiá zài nǎli?*

**李文麗：** 鹿港是以前台灣中部最繁榮的港口，據說河邊經常有鹿群出現，也有人說是因為港的形状像鹿角，人們便稱這個地方「鹿港」。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Lùgǎng shì yǐqián Táiwān zhōngbù zuì fánróng de gǎngkǒu, jùshuō hébiān jīngcháng yǒu lùqún chūxiàn, yě yǒu rén shuō shì yīnwèi gǎng de xíngzhuàng xiàng lùjiǎo, rénmén biàn chēng zhège dìfāng «Lùgǎng».*

**高來恩：** 台灣以前有很多鹿嗎？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Táiwān yǐqián yǒu hěnduō lù ma?*

**李文麗：** 以前台灣中部滿山遍野到處可以看到鹿。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Yǐqián Táiwān zhōngbù mǎnshān biànyě dào chù kěyǐ kàn dào lù.*

**高來恩：** 為什麼現在在台灣看不到鹿，只有在動物園才看得到？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Wèishénme xiànzài zài Táiwān kàn bù dào lù, zhǐyǒu zài dòngwùyuán cái kàn dé dào?*

**李文麗：** 聽說後來有人為了賣鹿皮賺錢，不知殺了多少鹿，在這種情形下，鹿的數量就減少了。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Tīngshuō hòulái yǒu rén wèile mài lùpí zhuàn qián, bùzhī shāle duōshǎo lù, zài zhè zhǒng qíngxià xià, lù de shùliàng jiù jiǎnshǎo le.*

**高來恩：** 鹿港有什麼值得參觀的景點嗎？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Lùgǎng yǒu shénme zhídé cānguān de jǐngdiǎn ma?*

**李文麗：** 鹿港的風光已經成為歷史，可是留下了許多古蹟，那里的寺廟和民宅都是古色古香的，非常值得參觀。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Lùgǎng de fēngguāng yǐjīng chéngwéi lìshǐ, kěshì liúxiàle xǔduō gǔjī, nàlǐ de sìmiào hé mínzhái dōu shì gǔsè gǔxiāng de, fēicháng zhídé cānguān.*

**高來恩：** 那裡有什麼好吃的東西？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Nàli yǒu shénme hǎochī de dōngxi?*

**李文麗：** 鹿港有一些傳統點心和小吃，像綠豆糕、牛舌餅和蚵仔煎，都讓人垂涎三尺。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Lùgǎng yǒu yīxiē chuántǒng diǎnxīn hé xiǎochī, xiàng lǜdòu gāo, niúshé bǐng hé hé zǎijiān, dōu ràng rén chuíxián sānchǐ.*

**高來恩：** 哦，那麼好吃嗎？我真想嚐嚐看。再來，該介紹一下「艋舺」在哪裡了！

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Ó, nàme hǎochī ma? Wǒ zhēn xiǎng chángcháng kàn. Zài lái, gāi jièshào yīxià «Měngxiá» zài nǎli le!*

**李文麗：** 「艋舺」，在台灣北部淡水河邊，是台北最早發展的地方，差不多兩百年前是它最興盛的時期，現在叫「萬華」。因為當時淡水河上都是獨木舟——獨木舟是一種小船，原住民稱獨木舟為「艋舺」，大家就把這個地方叫「艋舺」。

*Lǐ Wénlì: «Měngxiá», zài Táiwān běibù Dànshuǐ Hé biān, shì Táiběi zuì zǎo fāzhǎn de dìfāng, chàbuduō liǎng bǎinián qián shì tā zuì xīngshèng de shíqī, xiànzài jiào «Wànhuá». Yīnwèi dāngshí Dànshuǐ Hé shàng dōu shì dúmùzhōu — dúmùzhōu shì yī zhǒng xiǎo chuán, yuánzhùmín chēng dúmùzhōu wèi «Měngxiá», dàjiā jiù bǎ zhège dìfāng jiào «Měngxiá».*

**高來恩：** 我還是不懂為什麼「艋舺」現在叫「萬華」？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Wǒ háishì bù dǒng wèishénme «Měngxiá» xiànzài jiào «Wànhuá»?*

**李文麗：** 以前原住民用獨木舟從淡水河載運農產品來賣，日本人統治台灣的時候，因「艋舺」的台語發音與日語「萬華」(Manka)相似，所以改名「萬華」，現在台語還是叫「艋舺」(Bangkah)。

*Lǐ Wénlì: Yǐqián yuánzhùmín yòng dúmùzhōu cóng Dànshuǐ Hé zài yùn nóngchǎnpǐn lái mài, Rìběnrén tǒngzhì Táiwān de shíhou, yīn «Měngxiá» de táiyǔ fāyīn yǔ Rìyǔ «Wànhuá» (Manka) xiāngsì, suǒyǐ gǎi míng «Wànhuá», xiànzài táiyǔ hái shì jiào «Měngxiá» (Bangkah).*

**高來恩：** 原來是這樣啊！那萬華有什麼可看的地方嗎？

*Gāo Lái'ēn: Yuánlái shì zhèyàng a! Nà Wànhuá yǒu shénme kě kàn de dìfāng ma?*

**李文麗：** 雖然萬華沒落了，卻依然保留了许多古蹟、寺廟、老街，都值得去看看，會讓人覺得好像回到過去。路邊也有很多傳統美食，觀光客都喜歡去那裡。下次我們一起去吧！

*Lǐ Wénlì: Suīrán Wànhuá méiluòle, què yīrán bǎoliúle xǔduō gǔjī, sìmiào, lǎojiē, dōu zhídé qù kànkan, huì ràng rén juéde hǎoxiàng huíqù guòqù. Lùbiān yě hěnduō chuántǒng měishí, guāngguāngkè dōu xǐhuan qù nàli. Xiàcì wǒmen yīqǐ qù ba!*

### English

**Gao Laien:** Wow! Taipei's East District is so lively!

**Li Wenli:** This used not to be a lively place. A hundred years ago it was still countryside, with few people living here.

**Gao Laien:** Where was Taiwan's liveliest spot in the old days?

**Li Wenli:** Taiwan's political and economic center shifted gradually from south to north. There were three liveliest spots: Tainan, Lugang, and Mengjia.

**Gao Laien:** I've been to Tainan. I know that about four hundred years ago, the Dutch used Taiwan as a trading base in Asia. Tainan has plenty of historical sites — Chihkan Tower was built by the Dutch when they ruled Taiwan, a household name among tourist attractions. By the way, where are Lugang and Mengjia?

**Li Wenli:** Lugang used to be the most prosperous harbor in central Taiwan. They say deer used to gather by the river, or maybe the harbor shape looks like deer antlers — people started calling it «Lugang».

**Gao Laien:** Were there really that many deer in Taiwan?

**Li Wenli:** In the old days you could see deer all over the mountains in central Taiwan.

**Gao Laien:** Why can't we see deer in Taiwan now? Only in the zoo?

**Li Wenli:** They say later someone killed deer for their hides to make money. No one knows how many were killed. Deer became that much scarcer.

**Gao Laien:** Any sights worth visiting in Lugang?

**Li Wenli:** Lugang's glory days are long past, but it left behind plenty of historical sites. The temples and old homes all have a quaint, classical air — very much worth seeing.

**Gao Laien:** Any good food there?

**Li Wenli:** Lugang has some traditional snacks — mung-bean cake, ox-tongue biscuit, and oyster omelet. They'll make your mouth water.

**Gao Laien:** Oh, really? I'd love to try some. Oh, and tell me where Mengjia is!

**Li Wenli:** «Mengjia» — on the Tamsui River north of Taipei. It was the earliest developed area of Taipei. About two hundred years ago it was at its peak. Now it's called «Wanhua». Back then the Tamsui River was full of canoes — canoes are small boats. The aborigines called canoes «Mengjia», so people started calling the place «Mengjia» too.

**Gao Laien:** I still don't get why «Mengjia» is now called «Wanhua»?

**Li Wenli:** The aborigines used canoes to ferry farm produce down the Tamsui River to sell. When the Japanese ruled Taiwan, because the Taiwanese pronunciation of «Mengjia» sounded like the Japanese «Manka», they renamed it «Wanhua». Even today Taiwanese still calls it «Mengjia» (Bangkah).

**Gao Laien:** So that's how it is! What's there to see in Wanhua?

**Li Wenli:** Although Mengjia faded, it still has plenty of historical sites, temples, and old streets — all worth a visit. You'll feel like you've gone back in time. The roadside has lots of traditional goodies too, and tourists love going there. Next time let's go together!

## 短文 Reading 09-03

### 落地生根

老張在身強體壯的年紀去當兵，後來離鄉背井，飄洋過海，隨著蔣介石的軍隊來到了台灣。根據台灣的人口資料，1949年從中國大陸到台灣來的人口數目，差不多是一百多萬，大約佔當時台灣人口的百分之十三。自從1949年以後，台灣和中國將近四十年沒有任何往來，因此改變了老張的命運。

剛來台灣的時候，老張以為過不了幾年就能回去。他雖然思念家人，卻一直無法回老家，連通信也不可以，家人毫無音訊。過了幾年他離開軍隊，靠賣麵過生活。遠離了父母親與妻子，朝思暮想，等待了二十多年還是不能回去，他想大概永遠都回不去了。他覺得很孤單，經過朋友的介紹，在台灣又娶了一個太太，生了兩個女兒。沒想到來台四十年後政府開放探親，他終於可以回去看親人了。他到處打聽家人的消息，才知道父母親和妻子早已在文化大革命時過世了。當他聽到這個消息，不禁流下淚來。

他終於回去見到了離開家鄉時尚未出世的兒子。兒子的童年沒有爸爸，靠親戚過日子，吃了很多苦。老張看著第一次見面時已經四十歲的兒子，他的心裡既高興又傷心。雖然老張為兒子買了很多東西，並且留下一筆錢，但是兒子仍然不能原諒父親，他怪父親沒有盡到做父親的責任。這究竟是誰的錯？誰了解他們內心的痛苦和創傷？老張帶著遺憾回台灣了。

老張說：「生活在沒有戰爭的時代，要好好珍惜。我這一輩子最精華的時間不是在打仗就是在逃難，吃的苦實在太多。我已經習慣這裡的生活，不打算回去了，台灣就是我的家鄉，我算是落地生根了。」恐懼不安的歲月過去了，老張在這裡找到了平靜。許多跟老張有一樣遭遇的老兵，都有一段刻骨銘心的故事。

### Text in Simplified Characters

落地生根

老张在身强体壮的年纪去当兵，后来离乡背井，飘洋过海，随着蒋介石的军队来到了台湾。根据台湾的人口资料，1949年从中国大陆到台湾来的人口数目，差不多是一百多万，大约占当时台湾人口的百分之十三。自从1949年以后，台湾和中国将近四十年没有任何往来，因此改变了老张的命运。

刚来台湾的时候，老张以为过不了几年就能回去。他虽然思念家人，却一直无法回老家，连通信也不可以，家人毫无音讯。过了几年他离开军队，靠卖面过生活。远离了父母亲与妻子，朝思暮想，等待了二十多年还是不能回去，他想大概永远都回不去了。他觉得很孤单，经过朋友的介绍，在台湾又娶了一个太太，生了两个女儿。没想到来台四十年后政府开放探亲，他终于可以回去看亲人了。他到处打听家人的消息，才知道父母亲和妻子早已在文化大革命时过世了。当他听到这个消息，不禁流下泪来。

他终于回去见到了离开家乡时尚未出世的儿子。儿子的童年没有爸爸，靠亲戚过日子，吃了很多苦。老张看着第一次见面时已经四十岁的儿子，他的心里既高兴又伤心。虽然老张为儿子买了很多东西，并且留下一笔钱，但是儿子仍然不能原谅父亲，他怪父亲没有尽到做父亲的责任。这究竟是谁的错？谁了解他们内心的痛苦和创伤？老张带着遗憾回台湾了。

老张说：「生活在没有战争的年代，要好好珍惜。我这一辈子最精华的时间不是在打仗就是在逃难，吃的苦实在太多。我已经习惯这里的生活，不打算回去了，台湾就是我的家乡，我算是落地生根了。」恐惧不安的岁月过去了，老张在这里找到了平静。许多跟老张有一样遭遇的老兵，都有一段刻骨铭心的故事。

### English

**Taking Root Where One Lands**

Lao Zhang was still in his prime when he was drafted. Later he left his hometown, sailed across the sea, and came to Taiwan with Chiang Kai-shek's army. According to Taiwan's population records, about 1.9 million people crossed over from mainland China to Taiwan in 1949 — roughly 13% of Taiwan's population at the time. After 1949, Taiwan and China had almost no contact for nearly forty years, which changed Lao Zhang's fate.

When he first arrived in Taiwan, Lao Zhang figured he'd be back home in a few years. He missed his family, but he couldn't return — not even a letter could get through. No news at all from his family. Years later he left the army and survived by selling noodles. Far from his parents and wife, he yearned for them day and night. After waiting over twenty years he still couldn't go back. He figured he'd never return. He felt very lonely. Through a friend's introduction he remarried in Taiwan and had two daughters. Forty years after arriving, the government opened up family visits. He finally got to see his relatives. But when he went looking for news of his family, he learned that his parents and wife had already passed away during the Cultural Revolution. Hearing this, he couldn't help shedding tears.

He finally met his son, who hadn't been born when he left his hometown. The son's childhood had no father; he was raised by relatives and ate plenty of hardships. Seeing his son — already forty the first time they met — Lao Zhang felt joy and sorrow mixed. Although Lao Zhang bought his son plenty of things and left him a sum of money, the son still couldn't forgive his father for failing his duty as a parent. Whose fault was it? Who understands the pain and trauma in their hearts? Lao Zhang returned to Taiwan with regret.

Lao Zhang said: «Living in a time without war, one should treasure life. The best years of my lifetime were spent either at war or fleeing from war — the suffering was truly immense. I've already grown used to life here, so I'm not going back. Taiwan is my hometown now; I've taken root where I landed.» The fearful, anxious years passed. Lao Zhang found peace here. Many veterans who shared his fate each have an unforgettable story.

## 文法 Grammar

### I. 把 A 當做 B — to treat, take A as B — 09-05

**Function:** This pattern takes A to be B, making A in the role of B.

- 有不少人把買彩券當做一夜致富的機會。
- 有時候我們把體育館當做辦演唱會的地方。
- 風水學是把利用環境、改造環境當做一門學問。
- 沒想到他居然可以把垃圾當做材料，做成藝術品。
- 有些國家的人把欣賞藝術表演當做一種社交活動。

### II. 在…下 — under the..., due to the... — 09-06

**Function:** This pattern presents the circumstances, the pre-conditions of a given fact.

- 在學校的教育下，孩子學會了禮貌。
- 在冰冷的氣候下，所有的動物都躲起來了。
- 在高房價的情形下，年輕人都買不起房子。
- 在財政預算沉重的壓力下，政府不打算提高國民年金。
- 在母親辛苦的照顧下，他的身體越來越健康了。

**Usage:** The nouns that appear after 在 are almost always abstract, and 下 has no locational reference.

1. 在老師的鼓勵下，他的成績越來越進步了。
2. 在少子化的情況下，補習班、幼稚園都減班了。
3. 在看不到遠景的情形下，年輕人都不想生孩子。

### III. The Various Functions of 該 — 09-07

**Function:**

1. **該 Vaux** — ought to, should (short form of 應該)
   - 去「國家戲劇院」或「國家音樂廳」，該穿著整齊。
   - 我們在享受雲端科技的便利時，該控制自己，不要沉迷在網路世界裡。
   - 政府要鼓勵生育，給錢並不是一個好辦法，該改善整體環境。
   - 學生該好好學習。
   - 你該珍惜這樣的機會。

2. **該 Prep** — it is someone's turn to do something
   - 我好緊張，下星期一該我報告紅毛城的歷史了。
   - 你先在這裡等一下，該你進去的時候，他們會叫你。

   - 哥哥玩線上遊戲已經玩了一個小時了，現在該弟弟玩了。

3. **該 Det.** — the; said; that (formal)
   - 百能連鎖超商最近把業務重點轉向電子業。由於產品品質佳、款式美，因此該公司的產品都很受歡迎。

   - 台灣許多學生都想考上醫學系，因此該系是台灣最熱門的科系。
   - 政府提高地價稅。該政策影響到大多數的民眾。
   - 這次「雲門舞集」在國外的表演非常成功。該團體在台灣幾乎無人不知，無人不曉。

### IV. 自從…以後 — ever since... — 09-08

**Function:** The preposition 自從 presents a time reference, from which point an event began.

- 自從大學畢業以後，他就經營了一家網路公司。
- 自從有了網路商店以後，我們待在家裡也可以買東西。
- 自從她有了孩子以後，她的生活更忙碌了。
- 自從她認識了新的網友以後，每天一回家就打開電腦，進入聊天室。
- 自從他到了南美洲念書以後，他就有機會天天跟同學說西班牙文了。

**Usage:** 以後 可以 sometimes be reduced to 後, but not often.

- 自從他搬家後，就再也沒見過他了。

### V. Summary of the Various Usages of 幾 — 09-09

**Function:**

1. **Interrogative number** — how many
   - 上次有幾個人參加演講比賽？
   - 做一個麵包的成本要幾塊錢？
   - 寫一本書要花幾個月？

2. **A vague reference to numbers in middle ranges** — several, some
   - 我去過歐洲、亞洲、美洲一共三十幾個國家。
   - 他請了幾十個朋友去聽他女朋友的演唱會。
   - 王教授在英國住了好幾年，所以她的英文很流利。

3. **To play down a number in negation** — not many
   - 剛來台灣的時候，他以為過不了幾年就能回家鄉。
   - 他的形象不好，沒幾個人支持他。
   - 何小姐對那個工作沒興趣，沒做幾個月，就不做了。

### VII. 仍然 — still the same, same as before — 09-11

**Function:** The adverb 仍然 indicates that a situation remains unchanged, still the same as before.

- 百貨公司週年慶雖然有折扣，可是價錢仍然很高。
- 母親已經勸了他許多次，他卻仍然不聽。
- 王先生在生活上碰到很多困難，但是他仍然不放棄理想。
- 雖然現在科技發達，地震仍然是沒有辦法避免的天災。
- 政府已經提出多項鼓勵生育的措施，但是「少子化」的問題卻仍然很嚴重。

**Usage:**

1. 仍然 very often occurs in S2, accompanied by 可是，或者，or 卻.
2. 仍然 is formal and is freely interchangeable with its colloquial counterpart 還是.
   - 雖然他來晚了，我們仍然/還是玩得很開心。
   - 爺爺雖然年紀老了，仍然/還是很有精神。
   - 已經下課了，他仍然/還是捨不得離開教室。

### VIII. 究竟 — I wonder — 09-12

**Function:** This adverb expresses the speaker's desire to get to the bottom of something, some inquiry.

- 萬一發生核能災害，我們究竟該如何應變？
- 那個問題究竟要怎麼解決？
- 雲端科技究竟會不會帶來負面的影響？
- 台灣究竟有多少家便利商店？
- 李教授的理想究竟能不能實現？

**Usage:**

1. 究竟 is more formal, while 到底 is more colloquial (see Book 3 Lesson 11).
2. 究竟, like 到底, is incompatible with 嗎.
3. It can appear either before or after the subject, but when the subject is a question-word, 究竟 can only occur sentence-initially.
   - 究竟誰了解這個問題？ *誰究竟了解這個問題？
   - 究竟哪個環境比較好？ *哪個環境究竟比較好？

## 課室活動 Classroom Activities

### I. 古蹟與歷史

**Goal:** 能介紹自己國家的歷史和古蹟景點的由來。

**任務：** 學生輪流向同學介紹自己國家的古蹟景點的由來，在什麼地方，怎麼去？並向同學說明為什麼值得去參觀。學生可利用圖片或 PPT 介紹。

### II. 戰爭與歷史事件

**Goal:** 能談論戰爭及歷史事件對國家的影響及改變。

**任務：** 請學生回家先準備，然後向全班報告戰爭或歷史事件發生的原因及影響，將來會不會再發生？要如何避免？

### III. 說故事時間

**Goal:** 能根據自己所知，說一個完整的故事。

**任務：** 學生先回家準備，再向全班說一個自己國家的故事，或自己所知道的故事。

## 中華文化點滴 Bits of Chinese Culture

### Vestiges of Colonization in Taiwan

Bell fruit (蓮霧), sushi (壽司), sashimi (生魚片), a-gei (阿給).

In Taiwan there is a vegetable that originates from the western Asian side of the Mediterranean called 荷蘭豆 Dutch Bean (snow peas), which, as the name intimates, was brought in by the Dutch when they colonized Taiwan. The Dutch also brought in a fruit via Indonesia, something Indonesians call 蓮霧 tjam·bu" (bell fruit). Pronounced "lienbu" by the Taiwanese, the fruit later became known by its Mandarin pronunciation, Tianwu".

Taiwan was under Japanese rule from 1894-1945. There are also many Japanese foods that have stuck around since the Japanese colonial period: sushi ("壽司" in Chinese, and "魚旨鮮";。/寸 L "(sushi)" in Japanese); bento (compartmentalized boxes of take-out food, "便當" in Chinese, and "弁当" or EV (bento) in Japanese); sashimi ("生魚片" in Chinese, and "刺身" or "冷 L 及"(sashimi) in Japanese); oden (foodstuffs boiled in a soy-flavored soup broth, "關東煮" in Chinese, "黑輪" or "o·lien" in Taiwanese, and Z/'(oden) in Japanese); a-gei, a snack found in Danshui, to the north of Taipei (tofu cooked in oil, "油豆腐" in Chinese, but pronounced based on the last two syllables of the Japanese word "勤,奈 G 【于" (abura-age); and tempura, a common night market snack (there are both crispy and chewy versions of tempura, "甜不辣" in Chinese, and "天婦羅""天魅羅", "天顶；U'"(tempura), or 66T "(tempura) in Japanese; u天婦羅" comes from the Portuguese word "tempura").

In terms of relics, it was the Dutch who built the 赤披樓 Chihkan Tower (previously Fort Provintia) and 安平古堡 (Fort Zeelandia in Tainan), as well as 紅毛城 (Fort Santo Domingo) in Danshui. The following buildings, however, are all remnants of the Japanese colonial period. 總統府 The Presidential Office Building in Taipei was completed in 1919; it was the tallest building on the island and served as 台灣總督府 (the Office of the Governor-General of Taiwan). Just east of the Presidential Office Building lies the 台北賓館 (Taipei Guest House), which was built in 1920, and served as the house of the Governor-General. The Taipei Guest House, a name adopted in 1950, is currently a guest house for emissaries and is administrated by the ROC government. 國立台灣博物館 (The National Taiwan Museum, formerly the Taiwan Governor Museum) was completed in 1915 and built in the classic Japanese brand of western architecture. 臺大醫院 (The National Taiwan University Hospital) was built in 1916, then called the Taipei Hospital, and served as the first public hospital of Taiwan during the Japanese colonial period. It was the largest hospital in all of East Asia. The Taiwan government lists all of the above buildings as historic sites.

Colonization has left a mark on sport as well. The Taiwanese love of baseball is thanks to Japanese influence, and the history of baseball in Taiwan can be traced back to Japanese colonization. In 1906 the first formal baseball team 嘉農隊 KANO, was formed in Taiwan under the direction of the Japanese. There are also many place names that have stuck around since the colonization period: 西門町 Ximending, 松山 Songshan, 神岡 Shengang, 岡山 Gangshan, 金山 Jinshan, 清水 Qingshui, 白河 Baihe, 石岡 Shigang, 關西 Guanxi, and many others too numerous to mention. Lastly, it is worth noting that many of the elderly in Taiwan were born during the colonial period and thus brought up in the Japanese education system, and even today many can still speak fluent Japanese!
